# Reference Reasoning

## Material interpretation

The customer has reported a real service impact, but the case does not yet establish the failed component, contractual priority, coverage of the refurbished controls, purchase-order authority or a promised resolution target. The initial response must therefore progress diagnosis and protect the customer relationship while keeping those matters explicit.

The most defensible default is to verify contract/SLA facts immediately and prepare or dispatch Samir for a bounded diagnostic attendance once the coordinator is satisfied that the attendance itself is commercially released. Samir can inspect the maintained boilers, pumps and conventional controls, establish whether heat is available to the upper-floor zones, gather evidence and escalate. He must not be represented as an advanced BMS specialist or instructed to perform work beyond competence.

Ellie should be treated as a conditional follow-on or parallel contingency. Releasing her requires a service-manager and project-manager decision because it risks another commitment. Her attendance should depend on evidence that specialist controls competence is required, unless management deliberately prioritises the customer impact before diagnosis.

The customer should receive an acknowledgement, a statement of the checks and dispatch decision now underway, requests to confirm controls-riser access and the scale of impact, and a timed update. Frostline should not yet promise emergency classification, contractual coverage, attendance at a precise time, restoration before 15:00 or no-charge resolution.

## Minimum operational record

A suitable initial state is `triage in progress — coverage and competent response being confirmed` or equivalent. The record should include:

- next material action: confirm applicable contract/SLA position and commercially releasable diagnostic attendance while arranging engineer acceptance;
- accountable owner: named service coordinator until accepted handoff;
- dependencies: contract schedule/PO authority, priority assessment, controls-riser access and engineer evidence;
- review trigger: manager review at or immediately after 10:45, or earlier if customer impact or safety information changes;
- escalation: service manager for ambiguous coverage, specialist resource movement, unsupported customer commitment or chargeable work authority.

## Acceptable alternatives

A response may briefly delay physical dispatch while urgent contract and access checks are completed, provided it assigns a near-term deadline and does not let administrative uncertainty create unmanaged delay.

A response may request immediate management approval to reserve Ellie before Samir attends where it explains the commissioning impact and keeps the release conditional.

A response may recommend Samir and Ellie attend sequentially or overlap, but only where the authority, competence and displacement controls are explicit.

## Conclusions to reject

Reject claims that the prior controller reset proves the same fault, that priority-customer status automatically defines the SLA, that the annual PO authorises all reactive cost, that Samir can resolve any controls fault, or that an attendance before 15:00 can be promised from the supplied availability.

Also reject records such as `waiting for engineer` without a named owner and trigger, or `resolved` merely because an engineer has been dispatched.
