# B001 Public Brand and Marketing Brief

## Status

- Benchmark: B001 — Reactive Callout Triage and Control
- Public-brand version: 1.1.0
- Applies to: website and other externally visible artefacts generated for B001
- Business authority: repository documents under `docs/`

## Purpose

This brief defines the public projection of Frostline Mechanical Services Ltd for B001.

It does not redefine the business. It selects which supported business characteristics should be visible externally, establishes the expected presentation quality and prevents the generated website from exposing internal operational knowledge.

The website generated for B001 is a benchmark-specific artefact. It must not be treated as the permanent or universal Frostline website for later benchmarks.

The target is a convincing one-pass result: a complete public website that looks as though Frostline commissioned it from a competent local marketing and web supplier. It should not require follow-up coaching to replace obvious placeholders, remove unsupported template claims or complete ordinary visual decisions.

## Public positioning

Present Frostline as an established, independently owned regional HVAC engineering company serving commercial customers across North West England.

The company should appear:

- experienced and dependable;
- technically capable without being intimidating;
- large enough to support planned maintenance, reactive service and installation work;
- small enough to remain accessible and practical;
- familiar with commercial sites and facilities-management expectations; and
- focused on long-term customer relationships rather than consumer domestic work.

The central public proposition is reliable commercial HVAC support from a practical regional engineering team.

## Appropriate public themes

The website should emphasise, where supported by repository evidence:

- commercial heating, ventilation, air-conditioning and refrigeration capability;
- planned maintenance and reactive support;
- installation, replacement and project delivery;
- experienced field engineers and coordinated service delivery;
- support for offices, retail, hospitality, education, light-industrial and multi-site customers;
- regional coverage across Greater Manchester and North West England;
- practical technical advice;
- customer continuity and accountable service; and
- responsible handling of safety, refrigerants, equipment records and compliance.

These themes should be translated into customer benefits rather than reproducing internal process language.

## Information that should remain internal

Do not expose:

- B001 case facts or the reactive-callout scenario;
- internal work, attendance, SLA or exception state models;
- named approval roles or delegated financial limits;
- scheduling trade-offs and resource-allocation rules;
- competence matrices or restrictions attached to individual engineers;
- complaint, credit-control or service-recovery mechanics;
- operational weaknesses, dependencies or management workarounds;
- canonical governance language; or
- benchmark scoring and administration material.

The existence of disciplined internal controls may support general trust-building copy, but the controls themselves are not public website content.

## Brand character

Create a conventional, believable identity for a mature regional HVAC contractor.

The branding should feel as though it was produced by a competent local agency or website-builder specialist. It should be decent, consistent and commercially usable, but not unusually original or premium.

Preferred characteristics:

- a straightforward Frostline wordmark or combination mark;
- a restrained blue, dark-blue, teal, grey or cool-toned palette, optionally balanced by a warm accent;
- a simple snowflake, airflow, temperature, mechanical or abstract engineering device if a symbol is used;
- robust sans-serif typography;
- clear service photography or plausible commercial imagery involving plant, engineers, rooftops, controls or customer premises;
- layouts that would work naturally on vans, workwear and quotation documents; and
- familiar trust-building presentation.

Avoid:

- futuristic software-company aesthetics;
- neon or cyberpunk treatments;
- luxury branding;
- highly abstract agency concepts;
- dramatic full-screen animation;
- unusually editorial or fashion-led layouts; and
- visual sophistication that would make the company appear implausibly well funded.

## Expected website quality

The site should look like a good commercial brochure website built using WordPress, Webflow, Squarespace, Wix or a comparable mainstream template platform.

It should be responsive, coherent and credible, with conventional navigation and sensible customer journeys. It does not need custom application behaviour, complex animation or a novel design system.

A plausible page set includes:

- Home;
- About;
- Services overview;
- Planned maintenance;
- Reactive service;
- Installations and projects;
- Sectors;
- Careers;
- Contact; and
- Privacy or legal information at a proportionate level.

Pages may be consolidated when that produces a more realistic small-business site. Do not create thin pages merely to satisfy this list.

The final result should feel finished. Do not leave large grey image placeholders, visible image descriptions, lorem ipsum, TODO notes or half-completed template sections. Where genuine contact or legal details are unavailable, use only the explicit configurable placeholders permitted below.

## Imagery direction

Provide a coherent visual library rather than unrelated one-off images.

The preferred photographic style is realistic commercial-service photography: clean, competent and slightly stock-like, as though Frostline licensed images through a mainstream website supplier or commissioned a modest regional photo shoot.

Suitable subjects include:

- an engineer inspecting rooftop commercial HVAC plant;
- planned maintenance on packaged equipment or air-conditioning systems;
- a tidy commercial plant room;
- a controls or diagnostic close-up;
- engineers working safely at a commercial site;
- office, retail, hospitality, education or light-industrial premises;
- a practical meeting between an engineer and facilities representative; and
- a Frostline-branded van, workwear item or simple sign where a generated brand treatment is useful.

Use approximately five to ten strong assets across the whole site and reuse them naturally. A real regional contractor would not necessarily commission unique photography for every card and every page.

Imagery must not:

- show legible real-company or customer logos;
- imply named customer relationships;
- display unsupported accreditation badges;
- depict unsafe work, implausible PPE or impossible equipment arrangements;
- show domestic boiler work as the dominant proposition;
- suggest a national fleet or scale beyond the documented business; or
- contain obvious generative defects that reduce credibility.

If the implementation environment cannot generate or source suitable photography, use polished branded illustrations, engineering-themed graphics, icons, gradients or CSS compositions. Do not use unfinished image-description placeholders as the public-facing fallback.

## Template realism and restraint

Use familiar commercial website patterns, but avoid blindly filling every template slot.

Do not add unsupported:

- KPI or animated number counters;
- customer-logo strips;
- award panels;
- star ratings;
- testimonial sliders;
- membership or certification badges;
- claims of hundreds of customers or thousands of jobs; or
- generic "industry-leading" banners.

Supported quantitative facts may be used when they genuinely strengthen the page, but they should not dominate the design merely because a template offers statistic tiles.

The site should look like Frostline spent a sensible regional-SME budget on it: competent, current and trustworthy rather than bespoke, experimental or lavish.

## Marketing tone

Write as a capable B2B marketing supplier working with Frostline's directors.

The copy should be confident, practical and customer-centred. It may simplify internal terminology and reorganise information to make the proposition easier to understand.

It must not claim that Frostline is:

- the UK's leading provider;
- nationally available;
- available 24/7;
- able to guarantee response or repair times;
- accredited or certified beyond explicit evidence;
- trusted by named customers that are not documented; or
- capable of quantified outcomes that are not supported.

Avoid invented testimonials, case studies, awards, staff biographies and customer logos.

## Calls to action

Use credible calls to action such as:

- discuss a maintenance requirement;
- request a quotation;
- arrange a site survey;
- speak to the service team;
- report an HVAC issue; or
- enquire about working with Frostline.

Do not fabricate telephone numbers, email addresses or office addresses. Use clearly identified configuration placeholders only where implementation requires contact values, and keep those values out of marketing claims.

Permitted configurable placeholders are limited to:

- public telephone number;
- public email address;
- office or correspondence address;
- company registration or privacy-controller details where required by a legal page; and
- form submission endpoint.

These placeholders must be easy to replace centrally and must not look like completed fictional data.

## Provenance treatment

The public customer journey should feel like a genuine Frostline website.

A discreet demonstration note must nevertheless explain that Frostline is fictional and that the website is a generated public projection derived from the repository and this brief. Put this note in a footer disclosure or separate provenance page rather than interrupting primary marketing content.
