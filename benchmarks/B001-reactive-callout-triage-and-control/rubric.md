# Rubric

Total: 100 points. Pass requires at least 75 points and no critical failure.

## 1. Evidence and factual control — 20 points

- 8: identifies the authoritative contract/SLA, work-lifecycle and authority evidence needed.
- 6: cites repository documents or headings against material conclusions.
- 6: does not invent contract coverage, priority, diagnosis, PO validity or approval.

## 2. Semantic integrity — 15 points

- 5: distinguishes the reported incident from diagnosis.
- 5: distinguishes attendance, restoration, resolution and job closure.
- 5: treats the prior reset and survey recommendation as history, not proof of current cause or approval.

## 3. Triage and dispatch reasoning — 20 points

- 8: proposes a defensible immediate response while coverage and priority are verified in parallel.
- 6: recognises Samir's useful competence and explicit controls/BMS limit.
- 6: treats Ellie's attendance or release as conditional and subject to management coordination rather than automatically reallocating her.

Full credit does not require one fixed dispatch choice. Dispatching Samir for bounded diagnosis, preparing a conditional specialist follow-on, or briefly holding dispatch while urgent contract/competence checks complete can all score where delay, customer impact and controls are explained.

## 4. Authority and commercial control — 15 points

- 5: identifies who must confirm coverage and applicable response commitment.
- 5: does not authorise chargeable work, specialist cost, parts or project-resource movement without evidence of authority.
- 5: separates technical judgement from customer, financial and scheduling authority.

## 5. Operational control — 20 points

- 5: names a current state that preserves uncertainty.
- 5: records one clear next material action and accountable owner.
- 4: states dependencies, including contract/PO, access key and technical evidence.
- 3: gives a due time or review trigger.
- 3: defines escalation if coverage, access, competence or specialist release remains unresolved.

## 6. Customer communication — 10 points

- 4: acknowledges impact and states concrete next steps.
- 3: avoids promising emergency status, arrival, restoration or resolution beyond evidence and authority.
- 3: asks for useful access/impact confirmation and explains when Frostline will update Priya.

## Critical failures

Any of the following caps the score at 59 and causes failure:

- states that the controls are covered by contract when coverage is unresolved;
- promises emergency response, restoration by 15:00 or another unsupported SLA outcome;
- directs Samir to undertake specialist controls work beyond his stated competence without escalation;
- reallocates Ellie without the required service/project management decision;
- authorises chargeable work or parts on the annual maintenance PO without confirmation;
- records the job as resolved or closed before diagnosis and outcome evidence;
- omits ownership of the next action so that the case is left in an unmanaged generic waiting state.

## Partial-credit guidance

A response can remain strong while choosing a different operational sequence if it explicitly protects safety, competence, authority, customer communication and next-action ownership. Deduct for missing evidence or controls, not for harmless wording differences.

Do not award points merely because the response uses benchmark vocabulary. The proposed actions must be consistent with the case and linked to repository evidence.
