# Commercial Model

## Overview

Frostline earns revenue through installation projects, equipment replacement, planned-maintenance agreements, reactive service work and associated parts and specialist services.

The business aims for a balanced mix of recurring and project revenue. Projects can produce strong turnover and margin, while maintenance and service relationships provide continuity, customer retention and future replacement opportunities. This document uses the record meanings in [Canonical terminology and record relationships](../governance/canonical-terminology-and-record-relationships.md) and the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md).

## Revenue streams

### Installation projects

Installation revenue covers new systems, major alterations, refurbishments and customer-led capital projects. Work is normally quoted against an agreed scope and may be invoiced through deposits, staged applications, milestones or completion invoices.

Project pricing considers:

- equipment and materials;
- direct engineering labour;
- design and management effort;
- subcontract trades;
- access, lifting and logistics;
- commissioning and handover;
- risk and contingency; and
- required margin.

Large equipment purchases can create significant cash exposure before Frostline is paid, so deposit and payment terms matter.

### Equipment replacement and quoted repairs

Replacement work ranges from a single failed unit to a wider system upgrade. It often originates from service activity and is usually priced as a discrete quotation.

Quoted repairs may include known parts and labour, but diagnosis does not always reveal every defect. Quotations therefore state assumptions and exclusions where opening equipment or completing one repair may expose additional work.

### Planned-maintenance agreements

Maintenance agreements provide recurring revenue in return for scheduled visits and defined service commitments. Agreements may cover one site or a multi-site estate and can be invoiced monthly, quarterly or annually.

Pricing reflects:

- number and type of assets;
- visit frequency;
- expected labour time;
- travel and geographic spread;
- reporting requirements;
- contract administration;
- response commitments;
- included consumables or minor parts; and
- customer-specific risk.

Maintenance revenue is attractive because it is predictable, but poorly scoped agreements can become unprofitable if asset schedules, access assumptions or service expectations are wrong.

### Reactive work

Reactive work is charged through agreed call-out, labour, travel and material rates unless covered by a contract or warranty.

Some customers provide standing authority up to a spending limit. Others require a quotation or purchase order before any work beyond diagnosis. Out-of-hours attendance normally attracts different rates and is restricted mainly to contracted customers.

### Parts and materials

Parts and materials may be included in a fixed quotation, charged at an agreed markup or passed through under contract-specific rules. Freight, special delivery and restocking charges can also be relevant.

Frostline's value is not limited to the physical part. Identifying the correct component, sourcing it, checking compatibility, managing warranty and installing it safely are part of the service.

## Pricing approaches

Frostline uses several pricing approaches:

- fixed-price quotation;
- schedule of rates;
- time and materials;
- contract price;
- capped diagnostic or investigation charge;
- staged project valuation; and
- negotiated variation.

The company does not use one universal margin percentage. Commercial judgement reflects technical risk, customer relationship, competition, workload, payment terms and confidence in the scope.

## Quotations

A quotation normally records:

- customer and site;
- scope of work;
- equipment or materials offered;
- price and tax treatment;
- assumptions and exclusions;
- customer responsibilities;
- expected programme or lead time;
- warranty position;
- payment terms; and
- validity period.

Quotations may go through several revisions before acceptance. Staff need to know which version was approved and whether later correspondence changed any assumption.

## Purchase orders and authority

Many customers require a purchase order before work can be invoiced. The purchase-order value may cover one job, a monthly service period or several sites.

A purchase order is evidence of authority, but it does not always describe the technical scope accurately. Frostline therefore relies on the combination of quotation, correspondence, contract terms and purchase order rather than treating any one document as the complete agreement.

## Variations

A variation is a change to authorised scope, price or delivery conditions. Variations arise when:

- site conditions differ from the survey;
- the customer requests additional work;
- equipment selection changes;
- access or programme changes create cost;
- hidden defects are discovered; or
- another trade changes the required solution.

Where practical, variations are priced and approved before work continues. Managers may permit urgent work to proceed where delay would be unsafe or disproportionately disruptive, but the commercial position must still be documented.

## Credit and payment

Finance assesses new customers, sets account terms and monitors overdue debt. Standard terms vary according to customer type, project value and history.

The business may require deposits, pro-forma payment or staged payments where equipment cost or credit risk is high. Persistent late payment can affect future attendance, purchasing commitments and willingness to provide emergency support.

## Job costing and margin

Frostline reviews the relationship between revenue and delivery cost. Relevant costs include:

- employed labour;
- overtime and travel;
- equipment and materials;
- subcontractors;
- plant and access hire;
- freight and disposal;
- remedial or return visits; and
- unrecovered warranty effort.

Job costing is not perfectly precise. Engineers divide time across travel, diagnosis and several activities, supplier credits arrive later, and project costs may be shared across phases. The purpose is informed management, not fictional accounting certainty.

## Contract renewal and growth

Maintenance agreements are reviewed before renewal. Frostline considers asset changes, service history, labour consumption, geographic efficiency, customer behaviour and current rates.

The strongest growth path is usually through existing customers: adding sites, expanding asset coverage, winning replacement work and becoming the preferred contractor for future projects. Frostline values growth that deepens sustainable relationships over turnover won at structurally poor margin.
