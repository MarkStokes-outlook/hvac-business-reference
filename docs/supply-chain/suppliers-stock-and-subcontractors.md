# Suppliers, Stock and Subcontractors

## Purpose and scope

Frostline depends on manufacturers, distributors, specialist suppliers and subcontract trades to deliver work. The company does not manufacture HVAC equipment and cannot hold every possible spare part, so purchasing and external capacity are central to operations rather than back-office support.

This document provides the structural overview of Frostline's supply chain: the categories of external relationship, the types of stock held, and the role of subcontractors. Detailed purchasing controls, stock-management processes and material states are defined in their respective canonical sources:

- [Purchasing and supplier control](../procurement/purchasing-and-supplier-control.md) — purchase authority, requirement maturity, supplier approval, order management, receipt and acceptance, substitutions, returns, invoice matching and supplier performance.
- [Warehouse, stock and material control](../warehouse/warehouse-stock-and-material-control.md) — stock identity, available/reserved/quarantined states, picking and issue, van stock control, project materials, refrigerants, tools, counts, obsolescence and disposal.
- [Manufacturer relationships](../manufacturers/manufacturer-relationships.md) — technical support, warranty routes, product selection and manufacturer governance.

The Procurement and Stores Manager coordinates most purchasing and stock activity, but engineers, estimators, project managers and service coordinators all contribute technical and timing information. This document uses the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md).

## Supplier categories

Frostline works with several kinds of supplier:

- equipment manufacturers and authorised distributors;
- refrigeration and air-conditioning wholesalers;
- heating and plumbing merchants;
- electrical wholesalers;
- controls specialists;
- fabrication and ductwork suppliers;
- access, lifting and plant-hire providers;
- waste and refrigerant-recovery providers;
- couriers and specialist transport providers; and
- general consumables and workwear suppliers.

Some suppliers are used frequently under established account terms. Others are selected for a particular manufacturer, location, product or specialist need.

## Supplier selection principles

Supplier choice considers more than unit price. Frostline also considers:

- product suitability and compatibility;
- technical support;
- lead time and stock availability;
- warranty process;
- delivery reliability;
- account terms and credit limit;
- returns policy;
- manufacturer authorisation;
- prior performance; and
- relationship quality.

A cheaper supplier can create greater overall cost if delivery fails, technical advice is poor or warranty support is difficult.

Selection criteria, approval scoping and performance review are governed by [Purchasing and supplier control](../procurement/purchasing-and-supplier-control.md).

## Purchasing routes

Purchases may be made for:

- a specific installation project;
- a quoted repair;
- a reactive job;
- planned-maintenance materials;
- replenishment of warehouse stock;
- replenishment of engineer van stock; or
- general business use.

The intended job, project or stock purpose should be clear when an order is placed. Without that link, costs are harder to allocate and materials can arrive without anybody knowing who is waiting for them.

Purchase authority, requirement maturity states and order management are defined in [Purchasing and supplier control](../procurement/purchasing-and-supplier-control.md).

## Equipment lead times

Major HVAC equipment can have significant or variable lead times. Availability can change between quotation and customer acceptance.

Frostline therefore distinguishes between:

- indicative availability at quotation stage;
- confirmed availability when the order is ready to place;
- supplier-promised dispatch or delivery date; and
- actual receipt.

Project dates should not be treated as secure until critical equipment and delivery dependencies are understood.

## Stock holdings

Frostline holds commonly used service parts, consumables, fittings, filters, controls items and installation materials. Stock is intended to support delivery, not to become a speculative inventory business.

### Warehouse stock

Warehouse stock decisions reflect:

- frequency of use;
- supplier availability;
- consequence of not having the item;
- shelf life or obsolescence;
- unit value;
- storage space; and
- compatibility across the installed estate.

High-value or model-specific components are usually purchased for a known job rather than held routinely.

### Van stock

Service engineers carry a practical selection of common parts and consumables. Van stock improves first-time repair capability but is harder to control than depot stock because it is distributed and used in the field.

Engineers are expected to record material use and request replenishment. Exact van contents differ by engineer role, experience and the equipment they commonly support.

### Reserved and project materials

Materials purchased for a specific job may be physically stored alongside general stock but are not freely available for other work. Using reserved material for an urgent job can solve one problem while quietly creating another.

Where reassignment is necessary, procurement confirms replacement availability and updates the affected managers.

Stock states, reservation controls, picking, issue, van replenishment and material custody are governed by [Warehouse, stock and material control](../warehouse/warehouse-stock-and-material-control.md).

## Returns, credits and warranty parts

Unused or incorrect parts may be returned subject to supplier terms. Special-order equipment can attract restocking charges or may be non-returnable.

Warranty parts often follow a different process. A supplier or manufacturer may require fault evidence, serial details, commissioning records or return of the failed component before issuing credit or replacement.

A replacement supplied free of charge is not necessarily cost-free to Frostline; diagnosis, removal, return administration and repeat labour may remain unrecovered.

Warranty evidence custody is governed by [Warehouse, stock and material control](../warehouse/warehouse-stock-and-material-control.md) and cost-recovery treatment by [Warranty responsibility and remedial work](../warranty/warranty-responsibility-and-remedial-work.md).

## Subcontract engineers and specialist trades

Frostline uses approved subcontractors for additional capacity and specialist work. Common external activities include:

- specialist HVAC engineering;
- electrical alterations;
- controls integration;
- ductwork manufacture and installation;
- crane and lifting operations;
- scaffolding and access;
- builders' work; and
- water-treatment or other specialist commissioning.

Subcontractors do not replace Frostline's responsibility to the customer. The relevant Frostline manager remains accountable for scope, coordination, evidence, quality and commercial control.

## Subcontractor approval and competence

Before regular use, Frostline considers subcontractor competence, insurance, qualifications, safety documentation, references and relevant experience.

Approval is not permanent. Performance, expired documentation, customer feedback or changes in capability can affect whether a subcontractor remains suitable.

Approval status categories are defined in [Purchasing and supplier control](../procurement/purchasing-and-supplier-control.md) and apply equally to subcontractors.

## Subcontract instructions and evidence

A subcontract instruction should define:

- site and contact details;
- scope and exclusions;
- programme;
- agreed price or rate;
- required qualifications;
- safety and access requirements;
- materials responsibility;
- completion evidence; and
- invoicing expectations.

Verbal arrangements are sometimes necessary during urgent work, but they create risk if not confirmed. The handoff requirements in [Exceptions, handoffs and next-action control](../operations/exceptions-handoffs-and-next-action-control.md) apply to subcontract instructions.

## Supply-chain risk

Supply-chain issues can affect cost, programme and customer service. Common risks include:

- discontinued or superseded equipment;
- long or uncertain lead times;
- manufacturer-specific compatibility;
- damaged deliveries;
- supplier credit constraints;
- subcontractor availability;
- incomplete technical information; and
- parts ordered against an incorrect diagnosis.

Frostline manages these risks through experienced procurement, technical checking, supplier relationships and clear communication. No process removes all uncertainty; the aim is to expose it early enough for sensible decisions.
