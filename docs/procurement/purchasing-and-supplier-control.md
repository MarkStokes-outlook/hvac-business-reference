# Purchasing and Supplier Control

## Purpose

Frostline purchases equipment, parts, materials, hire services and specialist support. Procurement must balance technical suitability, availability, total cost, delivery risk, warranty position, cash exposure and customer commitments.

A purchase is a company commitment. The person who identifies a need, the person who specifies the item, the person who approves expenditure and the person who places the order may be different people.

This document follows [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md), [Canonical terminology and record relationships](../governance/canonical-terminology-and-record-relationships.md), and [Exceptions, handoffs and next-action control](../operations/exceptions-handoffs-and-next-action-control.md).

## Purchase requirement

A purchase requirement should identify:

- requesting person and accountable work owner;
- job, project, stock or overhead purpose;
- customer and site where relevant;
- description and quantity;
- manufacturer, model and part number where known;
- technical ratings and compatibility constraints;
- required date and delivery location;
- whether the item is for diagnosis, permanent repair, temporary repair or warranty remediation;
- acceptable alternatives and who may approve them;
- expected cost or budget basis;
- customer authority where cost depends on customer approval;
- Frostline expenditure authority;
- evidence-preservation or return requirements; and
- consequence of delay.

A photograph or informal message may support identification, but it does not replace a usable requirement.

## Requirement maturity

Procurement distinguishes between:

- **identification requested** — technical assistance is needed to determine the item;
- **price requested** — specification is sufficiently stable for supplier quotation;
- **approval requested** — price and terms are known but authority is outstanding;
- **ready to order** — specification, supplier, price, authority and delivery basis are confirmed;
- **ordered** — Frostline has issued a commitment;
- **supplier acknowledged** — the supplier has accepted scope, price and expected date;
- **dispatched**;
- **received**; and
- **technically accepted or quarantined**.

These states prevent “ordered” being used loosely to mean that somebody has merely asked a supplier a question.

## Supplier selection

Supplier choice considers:

- product suitability and provenance;
- availability and confirmed lead time;
- technical knowledge;
- delivery performance;
- total landed cost;
- credit and payment terms;
- returns and restocking terms;
- manufacturer relationship;
- warranty support;
- quality history;
- geographic coverage;
- ethical and environmental considerations; and
- resilience where supply is disrupted.

The lowest headline price may be poor value where freight, delays, incorrect supply, weak technical support or unrecoverable warranty cost are likely.

## Approved, conditional and restricted suppliers

Approved status is scoped. A supplier may be suitable for standard consumables but not specialist controls, refrigerants, safety-critical equipment or subcontract labour.

A supplier may be:

- approved;
- approved with category or value restrictions;
- approved only for emergency use;
- under performance review;
- suspended; or
- prohibited.

Use outside the normal approval scope requires explicit authority and recorded rationale.

## Quotations and comparison

Supplier offers are compared on a like-for-like basis including:

- exact specification;
- included accessories;
- quantity and unit of measure;
- carriage and delivery;
- lead time and confidence;
- validity;
- payment requirements;
- cancellation terms;
- warranty;
- returns;
- commissioning or technical support; and
- exclusions.

A supplier quote is evidence for a purchasing decision, not authority to place the order.

## Purchase authority

Approval limits consider value and risk. Additional review may be required for:

- deposits or prepayment;
- non-returnable or made-to-order goods;
- foreign currency;
- unusual supplier terms;
- new or unapproved supplier;
- long lead time;
- uncertain customer recovery;
- low-margin quoted work;
- safety-critical substitution;
- warranty cost awaiting recovery; or
- cumulative exposure across several related orders.

Splitting orders to avoid an approval threshold is prohibited.

## Customer approval and company approval

Customer authority to pay does not itself authorise Frostline to purchase. Frostline must still decide whether the commitment is commercially and operationally acceptable.

Likewise, internal budget approval does not prove the customer has agreed to the resulting charge.

Where the order proceeds before customer authority, the responsible manager records why Frostline is accepting that exposure.

## Purchase orders

A purchase order records the agreed commitment and normally includes:

- supplier;
- delivery and invoice addresses;
- job, project or stock reference;
- item, quantity and specification;
- agreed price and VAT basis;
- carriage;
- required or confirmed date;
- delivery contact;
- technical conditions;
- documentation or certification requirements;
- warranty and return references where relevant; and
- authorising person.

A verbal emergency order is documented promptly with the same essential evidence.

## Supplier acknowledgement

Critical orders are not treated as secure until the supplier confirms:

- accepted specification;
- quantity;
- price;
- expected dispatch or delivery;
- any back-order;
- substitutions;
- cancellation status; and
- special conditions.

Silence after issuing a purchase order is not positive confirmation.

## Lead times and expediting

Lead time may mean time to manufacture, dispatch or deliver. Procurement records which event the supplier has promised and the confidence of that promise.

Expediting checks the actual dependency, such as production allocation, inbound shipment, test completion, dispatch booking or courier movement.

Where a date changes, the work owner is informed with impact, confidence and available alternatives rather than only “delayed”.

## Substitutions

A proposed substitute is reviewed for:

- duty and performance;
- dimensions and connections;
- electrical characteristics;
- refrigerant or medium compatibility;
- controls and communications;
- compliance and certification;
- installation method;
- maintenance and spare parts;
- warranty;
- customer specification;
- programme; and
- price and margin.

Technical approval, customer approval and commercial approval remain separate where each applies.

A supplier cannot approve equivalence on Frostline's behalf.

## Direct deliveries

Direct delivery to an engineer, customer site or project requires:

- named recipient;
- delivery location and access;
- expected date and time window;
- checking responsibility;
- proof of condition;
- storage plan;
- allocation reference; and
- escalation if nobody can accept the goods.

A carrier signature proves a delivery event, not necessarily correct specification, complete quantity or undamaged condition.

## Receipt and technical acceptance

Receipt confirms goods arrived. Technical acceptance may require later checks, unpacking, documentation review or engineer inspection.

Goods remain quarantined where they are:

- damaged;
- incorrect;
- incomplete;
- of uncertain provenance;
- missing certificates;
- electrically or mechanically suspect;
- potentially contaminated; or
- subject to a warranty evidence hold.

Quarantined goods are not included in available stock.

## Supplier errors and returns

Incorrect, damaged or surplus goods are protected from use and linked to:

- original purchase order;
- receipt evidence;
- photographs;
- supplier notification;
- return authority;
- carriage responsibility;
- replacement requirement;
- expected credit; and
- next-action owner.

Where Frostline ordered incorrectly, the cause and cost remain visible rather than being reclassified as supplier failure.

## Warranty and evidence custody

Failed components may need to be returned under manufacturer or supplier procedures. Procurement coordinates with engineering and warehouse staff to preserve:

- item identity;
- job and asset relationship;
- failure description;
- photographs and test evidence;
- removed-part condition;
- packaging;
- return deadline;
- RMA or claim reference; and
- expected recovery.

A replacement part received free of charge does not prove full labour, travel, refrigerant or access-cost recovery.

## Cancellation and surplus commitments

When work changes or is cancelled, procurement reviews:

- whether the supplier order can be cancelled;
- cancellation or restocking cost;
- ownership of made-to-order goods;
- alternative use;
- customer recovery entitlement;
- storage and obsolescence risk; and
- financial provision required.

The operational job may be cancelled while the purchasing exposure remains active.

## Invoice matching

Supplier invoices are matched to:

- approved purchase commitment;
- supplier acknowledgement;
- receipt or service evidence;
- price and quantity;
- carriage and extras;
- returns and credits; and
- job, project, stock or overhead allocation.

A valid undisputed amount should not be delayed merely because another line is under review. Disputed and undisputed treatment is separated where practical.

## Supplier performance

Performance review considers:

- order accuracy;
- on-time and complete delivery;
- lead-time reliability;
- product quality;
- technical support;
- substitution quality;
- urgent response;
- returns and warranty handling;
- invoice accuracy;
- evidence quality; and
- communication.

Repeated informal rescue by a familiar contact should not conceal poor underlying supplier control.

## Ethical conduct

Employees disclose conflicts of interest and do not accept personal benefits that could improperly influence purchasing.

Rebates, incentives and project pricing belong to Frostline and are evaluated transparently alongside technical suitability and customer fairness.

## Open purchasing exceptions

Active purchasing exceptions identify:

- exact state;
- work affected;
- operational and financial impact;
- supplier action expected;
- Frostline owner;
- next chase or decision date; and
- escalation route.

“Supplier issue” is not a meaningful status.

## Operating reality

Urgent HVAC work often creates pressure to order from incomplete information. The discipline is not to remove urgency, but to preserve who specified, who approved, what was promised, what arrived and who owns the consequences when reality differs.
