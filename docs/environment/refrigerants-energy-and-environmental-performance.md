# Refrigerants, Energy and Environmental Performance

## Overview

Frostline's environmental impact comes from refrigerant handling, equipment energy use, transport, materials, waste and purchasing decisions. The greatest customer impact is often the lifetime energy and refrigerant performance of installed equipment rather than Frostline's office consumption alone.

Environmental claims must be specific and supportable. Replacing equipment is not automatically greener if usable plant is discarded prematurely or the new system is poorly selected, installed or controlled. This document uses the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md).

## Refrigerant responsibility

Refrigerants are selected, handled, recovered, stored and transferred in accordance with applicable requirements and manufacturer guidance.

Important considerations include:

- refrigerant type and global-warming impact;
- charge quantity;
- flammability or toxicity classification;
- technician competence;
- leak prevention and detection;
- recovery and reuse suitability;
- cylinder identification;
- records and customer documentation; and
- end-of-life treatment.

Engineers do not routinely top up a leaking system without investigating the source and deciding on an appropriate repair response.

## Leak prevention

Leak prevention begins with design, installation quality, correct materials, sound jointing, vibration control and commissioning. Maintenance then looks for deterioration, oil staining, pressure or performance changes and other evidence.

Repeated loss from the same equipment requires escalation. Continually replacing charge without addressing root cause increases cost, emissions and operational risk.

## Recovered refrigerant

Recovered refrigerant is identified and kept separate according to its condition and intended route. It may be suitable for return to the same system, reclamation or destruction, subject to technical and legal constraints.

Mixed or contaminated refrigerant must not be represented as reusable product.

## Energy performance

Energy performance depends on more than nominal equipment efficiency. Actual consumption is affected by:

- correct sizing;
- building fabric and internal gains;
- controls and set points;
- operating hours;
- simultaneous heating and cooling;
- maintenance condition;
- airflow and water flow;
- refrigerant charge;
- sensor accuracy;
- user behaviour; and
- weather.

Frostline avoids claiming guaranteed savings without a credible baseline, defined assumptions and a way to measure the outcome.

## Equipment selection

Selection considers expected load profile, part-load performance, refrigerant strategy, serviceability, controls compatibility, noise, lifetime support and end-of-life implications.

A high-efficiency product can perform badly if oversized, badly commissioned or operated against another system.

## Controls and optimisation

Low-cost improvements may include correcting schedules, dead bands, set points, sensor locations, simultaneous operation and unoccupied running.

Optimisation should preserve comfort, process requirements, indoor air quality and equipment protection. Energy reduction is not achieved responsibly by disabling necessary ventilation or allowing unsafe temperatures.

## Maintenance and environmental performance

Dirty filters, blocked coils, poor water treatment, failed sensors, leaking ductwork and incorrect charge can all increase energy consumption.

Maintenance findings that affect efficiency should be explained to the customer with evidence and proportionate recommendations.

## Waste management

Waste streams may include metals, packaging, filters, oils, electronic controls, batteries, refrigerant cylinders and removed equipment.

Frostline segregates and transfers waste through appropriate routes. Customer-owned removed equipment is not assumed to become Frostline property without agreement.

## Reuse and repair

Repair, refurbishment and component replacement may extend equipment life where safe and commercially sensible.

Reuse decisions consider remaining life, efficiency, refrigerant availability, parts support and risk of repeated failure. Keeping obsolete plant running indefinitely can be as wasteful as unnecessary replacement.

## Transport and logistics

Route planning, grouped visits, appropriate van stock and direct delivery can reduce unnecessary journeys. These decisions must still support response commitments and engineer safety.

Electric or lower-emission vehicles are assessed against range, payload, charging, towing and operational needs rather than adopted as a symbolic gesture.

## Customer reporting

Environmental reporting may include refrigerant additions and recovery, leak findings, waste transfer evidence, energy observations and improvement recommendations.

Records should distinguish measured data from estimates and avoid double-counting savings across several initiatives.

## Environmental incidents

Significant leaks, spills or incorrect waste handling are contained, reported and investigated. Corrective action addresses both the immediate event and the control failure that allowed it.

## Improvement priorities

Frostline prioritises actions using environmental impact, legal duty, safety, customer benefit, feasibility and cost.

A polished sustainability statement does not compensate for leaking systems, wasted journeys or poor commissioning. Environmental performance is demonstrated through operational decisions.
