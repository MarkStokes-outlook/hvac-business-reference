# Warranty Responsibility and Remedial Work

## Purpose

Warranty work is not a single category of free attendance. Frostline separates technical diagnosis, immediate customer support, legal or contractual responsibility, manufacturer recovery, subcontractor responsibility and internal cost treatment.

This document defines how suspected defects are assessed and controlled without prematurely assigning blame or hiding cost.

It uses the canonical record meanings in [Canonical terminology and record relationships](../governance/canonical-terminology-and-record-relationships.md), the authority model in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md), and the ownership rules in [Exceptions, handoffs and next-action control](../operations/exceptions-handoffs-and-next-action-control.md). Manufacturer warranty routes, registration states and claim processes are governed by [Manufacturer relationships](../manufacturers/manufacturer-relationships.md).

## Core principle

A report that recently supplied or worked-on equipment has failed creates a **suspected warranty case**, not an automatic conclusion that Frostline must absorb every resulting cost.

The business must distinguish:

1. whether a defect or failure exists;
2. the verified or likely cause;
3. whether immediate action is needed to make safe or restore service;
4. which party is contractually responsible;
5. which costs are recoverable and from whom; and
6. what evidence is needed to close the case.

Customer care may justify rapid attendance before responsibility is known. That service decision must remain visible rather than silently becoming an admission of liability.

## Sources of potential responsibility

A warranty case may involve one or more of the following:

- Frostline workmanship;
- Frostline design or equipment selection;
- manufacturer product defect;
- supplier damage, incorrect supply or delivery failure;
- subcontractor workmanship or design;
- customer operation, maintenance or misuse;
- third-party alteration or controls changes;
- unsuitable site conditions or utilities;
- excluded or pre-existing defects;
- normal wear, consumable replacement or maintenance need;
- accidental damage;
- force majeure or external event; or
- responsibility that remains genuinely uncertain.

Several causes can contribute to one failure. Assigning a single label too early can prevent recovery from the correct party.

## Warranty records

Frostline retains distinct but linked records for:

- the original quotation, contract or project;
- the installed or serviced asset;
- commissioning and completion evidence;
- the customer's defect report;
- the suspected warranty case;
- each diagnostic or remedial job;
- manufacturer or supplier claim references;
- costs incurred and recoveries received;
- technical conclusions; and
- final responsibility decision.

A remedial job can be operationally complete while the warranty case remains financially unresolved.

## Initial triage

Triage establishes:

- the customer, site, system and affected asset;
- reported symptom and operational impact;
- immediate safety or environmental risk;
- date of original supply, installation or previous work;
- whether the reported issue could be within an applicable warranty period;
- whether the customer has a maintenance obligation;
- any changes or third-party interventions since completion;
- availability of commissioning and service evidence;
- response commitments; and
- the owner of the next action.

The customer is told what Frostline knows, what remains subject to investigation and whether the initial attendance may become chargeable under defined circumstances.

## Attendance authority

A warranty investigation requires authority to mobilise labour and incur cost even when no customer charge is raised initially.

Authority may come from:

- an approved internal warranty-investigation limit;
- a service contract obligation;
- a manager's customer-care decision;
- confirmed manufacturer authorisation;
- a subcontractor call-back arrangement;
- customer agreement to diagnostic charges pending the outcome; or
- emergency make-safe authority.

The person diagnosing the fault does not automatically decide who ultimately pays.

## Diagnostic standard

The engineer records evidence sufficient to distinguish symptom, condition and cause. Relevant evidence may include:

- equipment identity and installation date;
- exact alarms or reported behaviour;
- state on arrival;
- operating conditions and controls demand;
- temperatures, pressures, electrical readings and flow;
- refrigerant or water-system condition;
- photographs and damaged components;
- commissioning settings and design conditions;
- maintenance history;
- environmental or utility conditions;
- signs of alteration, misuse or external damage; and
- tests performed and results.

A failed part is not always the root cause. Replacing it may restore service while leaving responsibility unresolved.

## Immediate restoration and evidence preservation

Where operational impact is serious, Frostline may repair or replace a component before every responsibility question is settled.

Before disposal or return, the team considers whether the failed component, packaging, photographs, controller history, refrigerant, oil sample or other evidence must be preserved.

The record explains:

- why immediate action was necessary;
- what evidence was retained;
- whether the repair may affect later investigation;
- who authorised expenditure;
- whether the intervention is temporary or permanent; and
- what follow-up remains.

A desire to restore service quickly must not destroy the only evidence needed for a manufacturer or insurance claim.

## Frostline workmanship responsibility

A workmanship defect may include incorrect installation, insufficient evacuation, unsuitable jointing, poor drainage, incorrect wiring, omitted commissioning, inadequate support or work departing from the accepted method.

Before concluding Frostline responsibility, the review considers:

- the accepted scope and design basis;
- original installation and commissioning records;
- elapsed time and intervening use;
- maintenance history;
- later alterations;
- failure mechanism; and
- whether the issue was reasonably discoverable at completion.

Where Frostline is responsible, the business normally acts promptly to correct the defect, communicate openly and record the direct and consequential cost. The purpose is service recovery and learning, not avoidance of an evidenced obligation.

## Manufacturer and supplier warranty

Manufacturer warranty normally depends on conditions such as correct selection, installation, commissioning, registration, maintenance, operating environment and claim procedure.

Frostline confirms:

- applicable warranty terms and duration;
- registration or commissioning requirements;
- evidence required;
- claim-notification deadline;
- whether prior authorisation is required;
- parts-return requirements;
- labour, travel and refrigerant coverage;
- exclusions and diagnostic charges; and
- likely credit or replacement route.

A manufacturer supplying a replacement part does not automatically reimburse Frostline labour, travel, hire, lost refrigerant or customer disruption.

Supplier claims distinguish product defect from transit damage, wrong supply, short delivery and commercial dispute.

## Subcontractor responsibility

Where a subcontractor may be responsible, Frostline preserves the customer's single point of contact unless the contract states otherwise.

The subcontractor is given:

- a clear defect description;
- relevant evidence;
- required response time;
- opportunity to inspect where reasonable;
- instruction about immediate make-safe needs;
- expected remedial scope; and
- notice that Frostline may arrange others if delay would worsen risk or customer impact.

Frostline does not delay urgent protection of the customer merely to preserve a subcontractor's preferred process. Equally, unnecessary third-party intervention should not prejudice valid recovery.

## Customer-chargeable outcomes

An attendance may become customer chargeable where evidence supports causes such as:

- no defect in Frostline-supplied work;
- customer misuse or unauthorised alteration;
- missed required maintenance;
- external power, controls, water, drainage or environmental conditions;
- consumable replacement;
- accidental damage;
- work outside warranty scope or period; or
- unrelated failure.

The decision to convert a warranty investigation to a customer charge requires three inputs: the engineer's technical conclusion (evidence that the cause is not a Frostline or supplier defect), an operational or account manager's confirmation that the customer should be informed and invoiced, and finance's agreement that the commercial basis and evidence are sufficient to raise the charge. No single role completes this decision alone.

Chargeability is communicated with evidence and reference to applicable terms. Frostline avoids surprising the customer with an unexplained invoice after repeatedly calling the case “warranty”.

Where the commercial position is sensitive, account management may choose a goodwill contribution. Goodwill is an explicit business decision and does not rewrite technical responsibility.

## Warranty status model

Useful warranty-case states include:

- reported and awaiting triage;
- investigation authorised;
- attendance scheduled;
- evidence required;
- awaiting technical conclusion;
- awaiting manufacturer response;
- awaiting subcontractor response;
- responsibility disputed;
- remedial action authorised;
- remedial work in progress;
- service restored, recovery outstanding;
- customer charge proposed;
- recovery received;
- internally absorbed; and
- closed with responsibility recorded.

A generic “open warranty job” does not explain the current constraint or next action.

## Cost control

Warranty cost includes all directly attributable value, including:

- engineer and management time;
- travel;
- replacement parts and freight;
- refrigerant and consumables;
- access equipment;
- subcontractors;
- temporary equipment;
- disposal and return charges;
- credits issued; and
- unrecovered manufacturer or supplier claims.

Costs are recorded even where Frostline deliberately absorbs them. Zero-price customer work is not zero-cost work.

Recoveries are recorded separately from costs so that gross exposure, recovery performance and final net cost remain visible.

## Customer communication

Customers receive clear updates covering:

- current operational condition;
- immediate safety position;
- tests or remedial work completed;
- temporary limitations;
- what remains uncertain;
- next action and owner;
- expected response or review point; and
- current commercial position where known.

Technical uncertainty is expressed honestly. Frostline does not imply confirmed manufacturer acceptance before a claim is approved.

## Disputes

A responsibility dispute is escalated with the underlying evidence, terms, chronology, cost exposure and desired decision.

The business avoids two equal and opposite failures:

- denying responsibility reflexively and damaging a valid customer relationship; and
- accepting responsibility casually, losing recovery rights and hiding recurring quality problems.

Material disputes may require director, insurer, legal, manufacturer or independent technical involvement.

## Closure criteria

A warranty case closes only when:

- immediate safety and operational actions are complete;
- the affected job or project records are linked;
- technical findings are recorded at an appropriate level of confidence;
- responsibility has been decided or the unresolved position explicitly accepted;
- customer communication is complete;
- remedial work is tested;
- supplier, manufacturer or subcontract recoveries are resolved or written off under authority;
- customer charges, credits or goodwill decisions are completed; and
- learning actions have owners where required.

Closing the remedial job is not sufficient if the recovery, dispute or root-cause action remains ownerless.

## Learning and trend review

Frostline reviews warranty cases for patterns by manufacturer, equipment family, component, engineer, subcontractor, installation method, site condition and failure cause.

The review distinguishes:

- genuine product failure;
- workmanship defect;
- design or selection weakness;
- maintenance-related deterioration;
- documentation or registration failure;
- supplier and logistics issue;
- customer misuse; and
- inconclusive cases.

Recurring cases may change approved products, installation standards, commissioning checks, training, supplier relationships, contingency levels or contract wording.

Warranty control is therefore not merely a claims process. It is a feedback loop connecting customer care, engineering quality, commercial recovery and future business decisions.
