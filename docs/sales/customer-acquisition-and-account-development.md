# Customer Acquisition and Account Development

## Overview

Frostline wins work through a mixture of referrals, repeat business, direct enquiries, manufacturer introductions, tender opportunities and relationships with facilities managers, property agents, consultants and principal contractors.

Sales activity is not separated completely from operations. Engineers, coordinators, estimators, project managers and directors all influence whether a customer trusts Frostline enough to award further work. This document uses the record meanings in [Canonical terminology and record relationships](../governance/canonical-terminology-and-record-relationships.md) and the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md). The handover from sales to delivery follows the ownership-transfer rules in [Exceptions, handoffs and next-action control](../operations/exceptions-handoffs-and-next-action-control.md).

## Types of opportunity

Opportunities commonly include:

- a first reactive call-out;
- a replacement recommendation following service;
- a planned-maintenance enquiry;
- an installation or refurbishment project;
- a multi-site service contract;
- warranty or manufacturer-referred work;
- subcontract work for another contractor; and
- a formal tender.

The route into the business affects how much information is initially available and how much qualification is required.

## Qualification

Frostline does not pursue every enquiry automatically. Initial qualification considers:

- location and travel implications;
- equipment and service type;
- required accreditations or technical capability;
- likely contract value;
- timescale;
- customer payment risk;
- access and working-hour constraints;
- whether the opportunity fits Frostline's commercial model; and
- whether capacity exists to deliver responsibly.

Declining unsuitable work early is preferable to accepting work that cannot be delivered safely, profitably or to the expected standard.

## Enquiry handling

A useful enquiry record captures the organisation, contact, site, problem or requirement, origin of the lead, requested timescale and next action.

The first conversation should establish what the customer is trying to achieve rather than jumping immediately to a particular product. A request to replace an old unit may actually arise from comfort complaints, high energy use, repeated failure, landlord obligations or a planned refurbishment.

## Existing customer development

Account development is primarily based on knowledge gained through delivery. Service history, recurring defects, ageing equipment, compliance gaps and changing site use can reveal legitimate future needs.

Frostline should distinguish between helpful advice and opportunistic selling. Recommendations must be supported by condition, risk, lifecycle cost or operational benefit.

## Account ownership

Larger customers normally have a named commercial or operational owner within Frostline. Ownership means maintaining the relationship and coordinating internal attention; it does not mean bypassing normal engineering, credit or approval controls.

Important account knowledge includes:

- decision makers and site contacts;
- procurement routes;
- invoice and purchase-order requirements;
- operating hours and shutdown restrictions;
- service priorities;
- communication preferences;
- estate plans; and
- known sensitivities or previous disputes.

## Sales stages

An opportunity may progress through:

1. identified;
2. qualified;
3. surveyed or investigated;
4. solution developed;
5. quotation or tender submitted;
6. clarification or negotiation;
7. won, lost, deferred or withdrawn; and
8. handed into delivery.

Stages should reflect reality. A quotation sent without confirmed funding or authority is not necessarily close to award.

## Tenders and frameworks

Formal tenders may require policy documents, insurance evidence, accreditations, references, method statements, pricing schedules and acceptance of customer terms.

Frostline reviews obligations before submission. A prestigious customer is not automatically attractive if the tender transfers unreasonable risk, imposes unworkable response commitments or requires unsustainable pricing.

## Pricing and negotiation

Negotiation may concern price, scope, programme, payment terms, exclusions, warranties or contract duration. Reducing price without changing anything else reduces margin and risk tolerance.

Where concessions are made, Frostline seeks a corresponding change such as reduced scope, improved payment terms, a longer contract, grouped visits or customer-provided access.

## Handover to delivery

A won opportunity must be handed over with the assumptions, exclusions, promised dates, commercial approvals, technical basis, customer contacts and unresolved dependencies intact.

Poor handover creates a gap between what was sold and what operations can deliver. Informal promises made during negotiation are still promises if the customer reasonably relied on them.

## Lost and dormant opportunities

Reasons for loss may include price, timing, incumbent loyalty, specification preference, procurement cancellation, capacity or failure to respond quickly enough.

Loss analysis should be honest. Recording every loss as "too expensive" hides weaknesses in qualification, responsiveness, presentation or customer confidence.

Dormant opportunities may be revisited when budgets renew, equipment condition worsens or a project restarts, but follow-up should remain proportionate and professional.

## Sales conduct

Frostline does not:

- invent urgency;
- disparage competitors without evidence;
- promise unavailable engineers or parts;
- misrepresent accreditation or manufacturer status;
- hide exclusions likely to affect the decision; or
- use technical complexity to pressure a customer.

Sustainable growth depends on work that Frostline can deliver well, collect payment for and support after completion.
