# Company History

## 2011–2013: Founding years

Frostline Mechanical Services Ltd was founded in 2011 by refrigeration engineers Martin Hale and Susan Mercer. Both had worked for larger regional contractors and believed there was room in the market for a smaller firm that combined competent engineering with direct customer service.

The company began with the founders, one apprentice and a rented industrial unit in Bury. Early work focused on small commercial air-conditioning installations, refrigeration repairs and subcontract support for larger mechanical contractors.

Martin led engineering and sales. Susan handled finance, purchasing and administration while also supporting site surveys and project coordination. Customers dealt directly with one of the founders, and nearly all operational knowledge lived in their heads, paper diaries and email accounts.

## 2014–2017: Establishing a regional customer base

Frostline won repeat work from hospitality, retail and office customers across Greater Manchester and Lancashire. It hired additional installation and service engineers, moved into a larger unit with secure parts storage, and began offering planned maintenance alongside reactive repair.

The maintenance service grew initially as an add-on to installations. Customers preferred to retain the company that knew their equipment, and Frostline valued the more predictable workload. Service records were maintained through engineer worksheets, shared folders and a central spreadsheet managed by the office.

By 2017 the company employed 16 people. The founders still reviewed most quotations and significant jobs, but responsibility for daily scheduling and customer queries had begun to move to dedicated office staff.

## 2018–2020: Broader services and operational pressure

Frostline expanded into larger replacement projects, ventilation upgrades and multi-site maintenance contracts. The company took on more apprentices and established regular relationships with specialist subcontractors for electrical, access and lifting work.

Growth created a practical divide between installation and service work. Installation projects were planned over weeks or months and depended heavily on surveys, quotations, procurement and coordinated site programmes. Service work was more reactive, with frequent changes to engineer schedules and a greater need for immediate access to asset history and customer instructions.

The business responded by creating separate installation and service teams under a shared operations function. This improved accountability but also introduced different working habits, terminology and local records.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, project work slowed temporarily while reactive and compliance-related work continued for essential customers. Office staff adopted remote working and improvised new ways of sharing information. Several of those temporary practices remained after normal operations resumed.

## 2021–2023: Acquisition and accelerated growth

In 2021 Frostline acquired the customer contracts, selected assets and five employees of Calder Cooling Services, a smaller contractor whose owner was retiring. The acquisition expanded Frostline's presence in West Yorkshire and brought valuable maintenance relationships, but the inherited records were inconsistent with Frostline's own.

Customer names, site references, asset descriptions and contract dates were migrated into Frostline's spreadsheets and shared folders over several months. Most active customers were reconciled successfully, but some historical information remained in archived Calder documents and personal knowledge held by former Calder staff.

The company appointed Daniel Mercer as Operations Director in 2022. Daniel had joined Frostline as a service engineer in 2014, later becoming Service Manager. His promotion allowed Martin to spend more time on major customers, commercial relationships and business development.

By the end of 2023 Frostline employed 36 people and supported more than 280 customer sites. Planned-maintenance revenue had become a core part of the business rather than an accompaniment to installations.

## 2024–present: A capable business at a growth ledge

Frostline has continued to grow through repeat business, maintenance renewals and referrals. It now employs 41 permanent staff and regularly coordinates multiple installation projects alongside planned service visits and reactive call-outs.

The company's operating practices have evolved incrementally. Teams use familiar tools and have developed effective local methods, but information is spread across accounting software, shared mailboxes, spreadsheets, shared drives, paper records, messaging conversations and supplier portals.

Experienced staff often know where to find what they need and who to call when records are unclear. New employees take longer to become effective because much of the business logic is learned informally.

The owners recognise that Frostline has reached a point where continued growth cannot rely solely on adding more people to existing coordination practices. They want to improve how the company works without imposing an enterprise bureaucracy that would undermine the responsiveness customers value.

Frostline's current position is therefore not one of failure. It is a successful regional contractor whose operating model has become more complex than the informal systems that supported its earlier growth.
