# Canonical Terminology and Record Relationships

## Purpose

Frostline's teams use overlapping words in everyday conversation. This document defines the canonical meanings used by the repository so that related documents describe the same business concepts consistently.

Informal language may still appear in quoted speech, customer communications or historical records. Where precision matters, the definitions below take precedence.

## Business parties and places

### Customer

The legal or commercial party that buys, contracts for or is otherwise responsible for work. A customer may own many sites and may use separate billing, procurement and operational contacts.

A property occupant, tenant, facilities manager or site contact is not automatically the customer.

### Account

The continuing commercial relationship with a customer, including agreed terms, contacts, credit position, contracts, sites and history.

“Account” is not a synonym for an individual site or invoice.

### Site

A physical location at which Frostline may survey, install, maintain or repair equipment. A site can contain multiple buildings, areas, systems and assets.

### Service location

The precise place within a site where access or work occurs, such as a roof plant area, kitchen, comms room or tenant unit. Service locations help distinguish several maintainable areas sharing one postal address.

### Contact

A person associated with a customer, site, supplier, manufacturer or subcontractor. Their authority must be established separately; being listed as a contact does not grant approval rights.

## Physical and technical concepts

### System

A set of interacting assets and components delivering a technical outcome, such as comfort cooling, chilled water, ventilation or heating.

A system may cross rooms, floors or responsibility boundaries and may include controls or third-party equipment.

### Asset

A maintainable item of plant or equipment that Frostline identifies individually because its history, condition, servicing or warranty matters.

Examples include an outdoor condensing unit, air-handling unit, boiler, pump or control panel.

### Component

A part of an asset or system that is not normally managed as an independent maintainable asset, such as a fan motor, sensor, valve actuator or compressor contactor.

A component may later be promoted to asset-level tracking if operational or contractual needs justify it.

### Asset identifier

Frostline's internal identifier for an asset. Manufacturer serial numbers, customer labels and site labels remain separate identifiers because each may be absent, duplicated, changed or recorded incorrectly.

### Serviceable item

A general term for anything requiring a defined inspection, maintenance, test or certification activity. It may be an asset, system, safety device or grouped set of equipment.

## Commercial concepts

### Enquiry

An expressed customer need that has not yet become authorised work. It may lead to advice, a survey, quotation, tender response or no further action.

### Opportunity

A potential sale that Frostline chooses to qualify and actively pursue. Not every enquiry becomes an opportunity.

### Estimate

An internal or preliminary assessment of likely cost, effort or price based on incomplete information. Unless expressly stated otherwise, an estimate is not an offer capable of acceptance.

### Quotation

A defined commercial offer describing scope, price, assumptions, exclusions, validity and terms. Acceptance creates customer authority only within the quoted scope and applicable conditions.

### Tender

A structured request for Frostline to submit a commercial and technical proposal under a customer's procurement process. A tender submission may contain several priced options and contractual qualifications.

### Variation

A documented change to authorised project or service scope, price, programme or responsibility. Discovery of additional work is not itself an approved variation.

### Purchase order

A formal customer or Frostline purchasing document authorising specified goods or services. A purchase-order number quoted informally does not prove that the order covers the actual scope or value.

### Contract

A continuing or project-specific agreement defining obligations, commercial terms and risk allocation. A quotation and acceptance can form a contract even where no single document is titled “contract”.

### Commercial release

The controlled transition from accepted opportunity to authorised work, confirming that customer authority, Frostline internal approval, credit conditions and scope understanding are sufficient to permit cost commitment. Commercial release is not a single approval; it requires both customer authority (external) and Frostline commitment authority (internal). Acceptance of a quotation may be necessary but not sufficient for commercial release where credit, deposit or internal margin conditions remain outstanding.

## Work concepts

### Work request

A request for Frostline to assess or perform work. It may originate from a customer, planned schedule, engineer recommendation, project team or warranty process.

A work request is not necessarily authorised or ready to schedule.

### Job

The operational container used to control a defined piece of service or small-works activity. A job may require several attendances and may produce quotations or follow-on jobs.

### Project

A managed body of work with a defined outcome, programme, budget and coordination requirement, normally involving installation, replacement or substantial alteration.

A project may contain many tasks, site visits, purchase orders and variations.

### Task

A discrete activity assigned within a job or project. Tasks do not normally carry independent commercial authority unless explicitly defined.

### Attendance

One physical or remote intervention by one or more people at a particular time. A job can have multiple attendances; an attendance can be unsuccessful without making the job itself invalid.

### Visit

An informal synonym for an on-site attendance. Repository documents should prefer “attendance” where the distinction from the wider job matters.

### Call-out

A reactive request requiring triage and potentially an attendance. The term does not guarantee immediate dispatch, contractual inclusion or successful repair.

### Planned maintenance visit

An attendance generated from a maintenance obligation or schedule. It remains a job or attendance operationally; “PPM” describes why it exists, not a separate physical kind of work.

### Follow-on work

Additional work identified from an attendance, survey or review that requires its own authority, planning or commercial treatment. It may be added to the existing job only where scope and controls remain clear.

### Return visit

A later attendance on the same job to continue already-authorised work, commonly because parts, access, time or specialist support were unavailable.

A return visit is not automatically free of charge or warranty work.

## Service outcome concepts

### Complete

The authorised operational scope has been performed and required immediate evidence has been recorded. Completion does not necessarily mean the job is financially closed.

### Partially complete

Some authorised work has been performed, but defined scope remains outstanding.

### Unable to complete

The attendance ended without completing its authorised purpose because of access, safety, parts, competence, time, information or another recorded constraint.

### No fault found

The reported condition could not be reproduced or verified during the available investigation. It does not prove that no intermittent or external fault exists.

### Restoration

Return to an agreed minimum service level, which may include an authorised temporary measure. Restoration is a distinct SLA event from resolution. A system may be restored (providing limited or backup service) without being resolved.

### Resolution

Completion of the agreed permanent remedial outcome. Resolution is not merely attendance, diagnosis or temporary restoration. It may depend on parts, customer approval, third-party action or design change.

### Temporary repair

A controlled intervention intended to restore limited service or prevent deterioration until a permanent remedy is approved and delivered.

### Make safe

Work limited to controlling immediate risk. It does not imply restoration of normal operation or completion of the permanent repair.

### Recommendation

A documented professional opinion about further action. It is not an instruction, quotation, approval or confirmed future booking.

## Commercial and financial records

### Cost

The value consumed or committed by Frostline, including labour, materials, subcontractors, hire, travel and allocated overhead where applicable.

### Price

The amount Frostline proposes or agrees to charge the customer, excluding or including VAT only as explicitly stated.

### Charge

A billable amount arising under a contract, quotation, schedule of rates or other authority.

### Invoice

A formal request for payment for supplied goods or services. An invoice may cover one or many jobs and does not replace the underlying delivery evidence.

### Credit note

A formal reduction or reversal of invoiced value. A credit note corrects commercial treatment; it does not delete the original operational history.

### Job cost

The accumulated cost attributed to a job or project for margin and performance review.

### Margin

The difference between revenue and defined attributable cost. Documents should state whether they mean gross margin, contribution or another measure rather than using “profit” loosely.

## Operational workflow concepts

### Triage

The initial assessment that determines impact, urgency, likely route and accountable owner for a new demand or exception. Triage is a decision, not merely a recording step. It may result in immediate dispatch, queuing, escalation, quotation or rejection.

### Handoff

The controlled transfer of accountability for an item of work from one person or team to another. The sender retains responsibility until the receiving person or duty role has accepted the handoff. A handoff must convey sufficient context for the receiver to act without requiring the sender to remain involved.

### Exception

A departure from the expected sequence where work cannot proceed under its current scope, authority, evidence, timing or ownership. An exception is not a failure; it is a recognised state requiring a decision or dependency resolution before progress can resume.

### Waiting state

A documented dependency preventing progress on an active item. A waiting state must identify the specific dependency, the expected actor, an accountable owner of the chase, and a review date or trigger.

### First-time fix

Durable restoration or resolution of a suitable reactive issue during the first attendance without an unplanned further engineering attendance. It excludes cases where return work was always planned, where investigation was the authorised scope, or where no safe and justified repair could reasonably be expected.

### Practically complete

The state where the intended operational outcome is available, subject only to recorded minor residual items. Practical completion is distinct from full completion and may precede financial closure, documentation handover or warranty registration.

### Priority

An assessed classification of urgency and impact that determines the relative order of response. Priority is a decision based on risk, contractual commitment, consequence and available resource, not simply a label supplied by the requester. It may be reclassified as facts emerge.

### Clock stop

A contractually permitted exclusion of elapsed time from an SLA measurement because a defined dependency prevents progress and the dependency is outside Frostline's reasonable control. A clock stop requires a recorded reason, the Frostline owner responsible for progress, the expected actor, and a chase or review point.

## Responsibility concepts

### Competence

The combination of knowledge, practical ability, experience, judgement and current authorisation that enables a person to perform a specific activity safely and effectively. Competence is assessed against the actual work being assigned, not as a universal grade. A person may be competent to perform one activity independently while requiring supervision or support for another.

### Owner

The person or team accountable for the next material action or outcome. Ownership should be singular at a given point even where several people contribute.

### Assignee

The person currently allocated to perform a task or attendance. Assignment does not necessarily transfer overall ownership.

### Approver

A person authorised to accept a decision or commitment within defined limits.

### Requester

The party asking for work, information or a decision. Requesting does not establish authority.

### Responsible party

The party contractually or operationally expected to bear an obligation or cost. Responsibility may remain disputed until evidence is reviewed.

### Escalation

An action that increases the decision authority, expertise or operational attention applied to an issue. Escalation is not a status label; it is a traceable event that records who is now expected to decide or act, and what triggered the escalation.

## Record relationships

The common relationship chain is:

**customer → account → site → system → asset**

Work records typically relate as:

**enquiry or planned requirement → work request → quotation or contract authority → job or project → task → attendance → evidence → invoice or warranty treatment**

This is a relationship model, not a mandatory straight-line process. Examples of valid variation include:

- a contract schedule creating a job without a new quotation;
- an attendance producing a quotation for follow-on work;
- one project covering several sites;
- one invoice consolidating several jobs;
- a warranty case referring to earlier jobs and manufacturer records; and
- one system containing assets maintained under different responsibilities.

## Identity and duplicate records

Records that appear similar must not be merged solely on name, address or description. Frostline may encounter:

- trading names differing from legal entities;
- multiple customers at one site;
- one customer with several billing accounts;
- replaced assets retaining old labels;
- duplicate serial numbers entered incorrectly;
- several jobs concerning the same symptom; and
- contacts moving between organisations.

Reconciliation should preserve source evidence, explain the chosen relationship and retain aliases where they are operationally useful.

## Status language

A status should describe the condition of one defined record, not the general mood of the work.

For example:

- a job may be **waiting for customer approval**;
- a purchase order may be **awaiting supplier acknowledgement**;
- an attendance may be **complete**;
- an invoice may be **disputed**; and
- the overall customer issue may remain **unresolved**.

Using one word such as “open” across all of these concepts hides the reason action is still required.

## Repository usage

Documents may add domain-specific terms, but should:

- reuse these definitions where the concept is the same;
- explicitly distinguish a narrower meaning;
- avoid inventing a new label merely for stylistic variety; and
- cross-reference the record or decision that gives a status its meaning.

Canonical terminology improves reasoning, but Frostline remains a real organisation: staff and customers will still use shorthand, legacy names and imperfect descriptions. The business model must preserve those perspectives without allowing them to silently redefine the underlying facts.
