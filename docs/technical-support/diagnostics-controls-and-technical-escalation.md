# Diagnostics, Controls and Technical Escalation

## Overview

Frostline supports engineers through structured diagnosis, access to equipment history, senior technical assistance, manufacturer support and specialist subcontractors.

Technical escalation exists to improve safety and decision quality. It must not become a substitute for gathering basic evidence or for developing engineer competence. This document uses the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md). Exception routing follows [Exceptions, handoffs and next-action control](../operations/exceptions-handoffs-and-next-action-control.md).

## Diagnostic principles

Diagnosis aims to identify the verified cause of a problem, not merely the component that currently shows an alarm.

Engineers work from symptoms and evidence, considering:

- customer description;
- equipment state on arrival;
- alarms and fault history;
- operating conditions;
- electrical supply and controls demand;
- temperatures, pressures and flows;
- sensors and interlocks;
- previous work;
- environmental conditions; and
- manufacturer information.

Parts should not be replaced solely because they are associated with a fault code. A code may identify the detected condition rather than its root cause.

## Safe fault finding

Diagnostic work remains subject to isolation, access, electrical, pressure, refrigerant and moving-machinery controls.

Live testing is performed only where necessary, by competent people using suitable equipment and precautions. The desire to obtain a reading does not justify uncontrolled exposure.

## Evidence before escalation

Before requesting technical support, the engineer should provide as much relevant information as reasonably available, including:

- manufacturer, model and serial number;
- exact alarm or symptom;
- sequence of operation;
- readings and test conditions;
- photographs or diagrams;
- work already attempted;
- recent changes;
- site constraints; and
- immediate safety status.

"It still does not work" is not enough for useful remote support.

## Levels of escalation

Escalation may progress through:

1. job history and internal reference information;
2. discussion with a senior engineer;
3. remote review by a technical lead;
4. on-site support or joint attendance;
5. manufacturer technical support;
6. controls, electrical or other specialist support; and
7. formal engineering investigation.

The appropriate route depends on urgency, risk, equipment type and available evidence.

## Remote technical support

Remote support can guide tests, interpret readings and help plan next actions. The engineer on site remains responsible for local safety and must not follow an instruction they believe is unsafe or outside competence.

Advice and resulting decisions are recorded where they materially affect diagnosis or repair.

## Controls and BMS interfaces

HVAC faults often involve the boundary between mechanical equipment, local controls and a building-management system.

Checks may include:

- enable signals;
- schedules and occupancy modes;
- set points and dead bands;
- sensor values;
- interlocks;
- network communication;
- actuator commands and feedback;
- alarm routing;
- overrides; and
- responsibility boundaries.

A unit that does not run may be operating correctly in response to an absent enable. Equally, a BMS alarm may be caused by a local equipment fault.

Frostline avoids altering customer controls without understanding the wider sequence and obtaining appropriate authority.

## Temporary measures

Temporary measures may restore partial service or protect equipment while a permanent solution is arranged. Examples include controlled manual operation, temporary cooling or heating, isolation of one circuit or revised operating hours.

Temporary measures require documented limits, customer understanding and a follow-up plan. An undocumented override can become a hidden permanent condition.

## Manufacturer support

Manufacturer support may provide fault interpretation, firmware guidance, part identification, warranty direction or field attendance.

Frostline records case references and advice. Manufacturer guidance is considered alongside site evidence and professional judgement; it does not transfer responsibility for safe execution.

## Specialist involvement

Specialists may be required for controls programming, power quality, water treatment, structural access, vibration, acoustics or forensic failure analysis.

The scope of the specialist's work and the question they are being asked to answer should be clear. Sending several specialists without a coordinated diagnostic plan can increase cost without resolving the fault.

## Complex and intermittent faults

Intermittent faults may require trend data, repeated observation or controlled testing. Closing the job because equipment happened to run during attendance can conceal the underlying problem.

The investigation may compare time, load, weather, occupancy, alarm history and control states to identify a pattern.

## Technical decisions and commercial authority

An engineer may identify the technically preferred solution without having authority to commit the customer or Frostline to the cost.

Diagnosis, recommendation, customer approval and purchase authority remain distinct decisions. Emergency safety work may proceed under separate authority.

## Learning from difficult faults

Significant diagnostic cases are reviewed where they reveal reusable knowledge, documentation gaps, product weaknesses or training needs.

Useful learning describes the symptoms, misleading indicators, verified cause, evidence and successful resolution. A collection of unexplained fixes teaches very little.

## Quality of diagnosis

Good diagnosis is demonstrated by a coherent explanation supported by evidence and confirmed, where practical, by successful testing after repair.

Fast diagnosis is valuable, but certainty should not be invented to appear decisive. Professional engineers distinguish what is known, what is likely and what still needs to be tested.
