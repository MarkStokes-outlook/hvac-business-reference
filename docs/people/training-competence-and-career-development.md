# Training, Competence and Career Development

## Overview

Frostline treats competence as a combination of knowledge, practical ability, experience, judgement and current authorisation. Holding a certificate may be necessary for some work, but it does not by itself prove that a person is competent for every task covered by that certificate.

Training supports safety, service quality, employee development, succession planning and the business's ability to adopt new equipment and working methods. This document uses the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md).

## Competence requirements

Competence is considered against the actual work being assigned. Relevant factors include:

- equipment type and capacity;
- refrigerant, fuel or energy source;
- electrical and controls complexity;
- pressure and temperature hazards;
- access conditions;
- customer environment;
- legal or scheme requirements;
- diagnostic difficulty;
- need for supervision; and
- consequences of error.

An engineer may be fully competent for routine split-system service but require support for large VRF commissioning, combustion analysis, complex BMS integration or specialist refrigeration.

## Sources of competence

Competence may be developed through:

- apprenticeships;
- formal college or training-centre courses;
- manufacturer training;
- supervised field experience;
- mentoring;
- toolbox talks;
- technical briefings;
- incident and defect reviews;
- self-directed study; and
- assessed practical work.

Frostline values practical learning but does not rely on informal experience where formal qualification or authorisation is required.

## Competence records

Records normally include:

- qualifications and certificates;
- expiry or renewal dates;
- manufacturer courses;
- equipment familiarisation;
- internal assessments;
- supervised-work evidence;
- restrictions or development needs;
- toolbox talks attended; and
- role-specific authorisations.

Records must distinguish attendance from successful assessment and current competence from historical exposure.

## Expiring qualifications

Renewals are planned before expiry. The lead time should allow for course availability, assessment, resits and operational cover.

An expired qualification may prevent particular work from being assigned even where the engineer remains technically experienced. Commercial pressure does not override legal or scheme requirements.

## New starters

Induction covers company expectations, safety, reporting, vehicles, tools, customer conduct, escalation and the limits of authority.

New engineers are not assumed to be fully deployable because they performed similar work elsewhere. Frostline verifies qualifications, observes practical performance and introduces work in proportion to demonstrated competence.

## Apprentices and improvers

Apprentices and improvers work under planned supervision. Productive work is part of development, but they are not used as inexpensive substitutes for competent engineers.

Development should expose them progressively to installation, service, fault finding, documentation, customer communication and safe decision making.

The supervising engineer remains accountable for the work within the supervision arrangement.

## Manufacturer and product training

Manufacturer training supports correct installation, commissioning, diagnostics and warranty handling. It may also be required to retain approved-installer status.

Product training is assessed for relevance. Attendance is prioritised where Frostline supports a meaningful installed base, expects future demand or needs competence for a specific project.

## Technical mentoring

Senior engineers provide remote and on-site support, but mentoring should build capability rather than create permanent dependence.

Where an engineer repeatedly needs help with the same task, the response should include structured development, not only another telephone rescue.

## Career pathways

Possible progression includes:

- apprentice to improver;
- improver to engineer;
- engineer to senior or lead engineer;
- specialist technical roles;
- commissioning;
- estimating;
- project management;
- service supervision;
- training or quality roles; and
- operational management.

Progression is based on role capability and business need, not solely length of service.

## Performance and development reviews

Reviews consider technical work, safety, documentation, customer conduct, reliability, teamwork and development goals.

Useful development objectives are specific, such as completing supervised VRF commissioning on three projects or improving fault reports to include verified cause, readings and recommended action.

## Training priorities

Training demand is prioritised using:

- legal and certification deadlines;
- safety risk;
- known competence gaps;
- business strategy;
- upcoming work;
- recurring quality failures;
- manufacturer requirements; and
- individual career plans.

Interesting training is not always urgent training. Capacity and cost require choices.

## Authorisation and restriction

Frostline may authorise an employee for some tasks while restricting others. Restrictions are communicated clearly to coordinators and supervisors so unsuitable work is not assigned accidentally.

Engineers are expected to recognise their own limits and request support. Escalating unfamiliar work is evidence of professional judgement, not weakness.

## Training effectiveness

Completion numbers alone do not show whether training worked. Frostline looks for improved practical performance, fewer repeat errors, stronger reports, safer decisions and increased independent capability.

Training that does not change workplace performance may need reinforcement, supervision or a different learning approach.
