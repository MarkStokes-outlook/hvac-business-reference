# Roles, Skills and Availability

## Purpose

Frostline's capacity is not measured only by headcount. Work can be assigned safely and profitably only when the available person has the right technical competence, authorisation, customer familiarity, location and working-time availability. This document uses the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md).

## Employment and engagement types

The workforce includes directors, office staff, managers, directly employed engineers, apprentices and approved subcontractors. A person may perform more than one operational role, particularly during busy periods or when covering absence.

Employment status does not determine technical authority. A subcontract engineer may be highly experienced but approved only for a defined scope, while an employed apprentice may attend many sites but work under supervision.

## Engineering disciplines

Field capability is grouped broadly into:

- air-conditioning and heat-pump service;
- refrigeration fault diagnosis;
- ventilation installation and maintenance;
- commercial heating and associated controls;
- installation, pipework and equipment replacement;
- electrical testing within agreed competence;
- controls diagnosis and integration;
- commissioning; and
- supervisory and project-lead capability.

These categories overlap. Frostline does not assume that every engineer is interchangeable.

## Competence and authorisation

Competence is evidenced through qualifications, training, experience, manufacturer courses, observed work and manager judgement. Some activities require formal certification or company authorisation.

Relevant records may include:

- refrigerant-handling certification;
- gas-related competence where applicable;
- electrical qualifications or defined limits of electrical work;
- working-at-height and access-equipment training;
- asbestos-awareness training;
- first-aid training;
- manufacturer accreditations;
- site-specific inductions;
- driving eligibility; and
- internal approval for lone working, on-call duty or supervision.

A qualification having once been held does not mean it remains current. Expiry, suspension, restrictions and evidence awaiting renewal all matter.

## Skill levels

Frostline informally distinguishes between engineers who can assist, complete routine work, diagnose independently, lead complex work and supervise others. These levels are contextual rather than a single universal grade.

An engineer may lead split-system installations confidently but require support on large refrigeration faults. Another may be the preferred diagnostic engineer for controls but not normally lead construction-site installation work.

## Apprentices and supervision

Apprentices are assigned work appropriate to their stage of development. They may travel with experienced engineers, assist on installation projects or complete limited activities under defined supervision.

Supervision can be direct, where the responsible engineer is present, or structured but remote for work that the apprentice has been authorised to perform. The supervising person remains accountable for ensuring the arrangement is suitable.

## Availability

A person's availability is affected by more than contracted hours. Operational planning considers:

- working pattern and normal start location;
- annual leave, sickness and training;
- existing appointments and expected job duration;
- travel time;
- van and equipment availability;
- on-call rota commitments;
- working-time and rest requirements;
- site-access windows; and
- whether the engineer can be interrupted from current work.

An apparently free diary slot may therefore not represent usable capacity.

## Geographic planning

Frostline tries to group work geographically, but urgency and specialist skill frequently override route efficiency. Engineers may start from home, the depot, a supplier branch or a continuing project site.

The nearest engineer is not necessarily the right engineer. Dispatch decisions balance response time, competence, contractual priority, likely parts requirements and disruption to already-promised work.

## On-call arrangements

Selected service engineers participate in an out-of-hours rota. On-call status does not mean every reported problem receives immediate attendance. The engineer assesses safety, contractual entitlement, business impact and whether a temporary action can reasonably wait until normal hours.

The rota requires a primary engineer and an escalation route. Managers may arrange additional support for serious incidents, unfamiliar equipment or simultaneous emergencies.

## Subcontractor approval

Subcontractors are approved for specified disciplines and may be restricted by geography, customer, project type or supervision requirement. Approval considers competence evidence, insurance, rates, availability, safety performance and previous delivery.

Use of a familiar subcontractor does not remove the need to confirm the scope, commercial basis and responsibility for records.

## Performance and development

Managers review technical quality, productivity, customer conduct, record completion, vehicle care, safety behaviour and commercial judgement. Performance cannot be judged from completed job count alone: difficult diagnosis, mentoring, customer retention and prevention of repeat visits are also valuable.

Training needs arise from expiring certification, new equipment, observed gaps, incidents, manufacturer requirements and business plans. Training removes capacity in the short term but protects capability in the longer term.

## Current operating reality

Managers carry a detailed mental model of who is good at what. Parts of that knowledge exist in certificates, training records and diaries, but practical capability, customer trust and restrictions are not captured consistently in one place.

This makes experienced coordinators highly effective and difficult to replace. It also creates risk when work is assigned from a simplified view of availability without the contextual knowledge behind it.
