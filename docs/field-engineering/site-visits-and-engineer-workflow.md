# Site Visits and Engineer Workflow

## Purpose

Field engineering is where Frostline's planning, technical competence, safety controls, commercial authority and customer service meet. Engineers are expected to work methodically and leave evidence that allows another person to understand what happened without relying on memory.

This document treats a site visit as an **attendance** within a wider job or project. Completing the attendance does not automatically complete the job, resolve the customer issue, establish warranty responsibility or authorise an invoice.

It uses [Canonical terminology and record relationships](../governance/canonical-terminology-and-record-relationships.md), [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md), and [Exceptions, handoffs and next-action control](../operations/exceptions-handoffs-and-next-action-control.md).

## Before travel

Before attendance, the engineer reviews available:

- customer, site and service-location details;
- contact and access instructions;
- work request and reported symptom;
- job or project scope;
- asset and system history;
- previous attendance notes;
- quotation, contract entitlement or repair limit;
- priority and customer commitments;
- known hazards and method information;
- permits, inductions and shutdown arrangements;
- parts, materials, tools and test equipment;
- expected duration; and
- later commitments that coordination may need to protect.

The engineer raises material gaps before travel where practical. Proceeding with known uncertainty may still be justified for emergency diagnosis, but the uncertainty should be deliberate rather than invisible.

## Dispatch acceptance

Receiving an assignment does not require an engineer to accept work silently where:

- the scope is unclear;
- competence or authorisation is insufficient;
- necessary equipment is unavailable;
- safe travel or working time is doubtful;
- access has not been prepared;
- the planned duration is clearly unrealistic; or
- the instruction conflicts with another active responsibility.

Concerns are escalated promptly. Quietly attending and hoping the problem disappears transfers avoidable risk to the customer and business.

## Vehicle and equipment preparation

The engineer confirms suitable:

- PPE;
- hand and power tools;
- calibrated test equipment;
- lifting or access aids;
- consumables and refrigerant equipment;
- expected parts;
- documentation; and
- means of preserving removed evidence where warranty or failure investigation may apply.

A recorded item on a van list is not assumed physically present or serviceable when the work depends on it.

## Arrival and access

On arrival, the engineer:

- parks safely and lawfully;
- reports to the agreed contact;
- identifies themselves and Frostline;
- confirms the intended attendance purpose;
- signs in and completes required induction;
- checks local hazards and rules;
- confirms access to the actual service location; and
- establishes whether operating conditions allow meaningful diagnosis or work.

Arrival at the site boundary does not necessarily mean the engineer can begin. Access delay and its owner are recorded where they affect SLA or charging treatment.

## Dynamic risk assessment

The engineer compares actual conditions with the planned method, considering height, heat, confined space, electrical energy, pressure, moving plant, refrigerant, gas, asbestos information, public areas, lone working and environmental conditions.

Any person may stop work where they reasonably believe it is unsafe or outside competence. Restart requires review of the relevant method, control, competence or authority.

Customer urgency and programme pressure do not weaken this right.

## Isolation and permits

Isolation follows the energy sources and site rules involved. Engineers identify the correct equipment, isolate, secure, verify and control stored energy before intrusive work.

The engineer distinguishes:

- customer permission to shut down;
- site permit authority;
- technical competence to isolate; and
- Frostline authority to perform the work.

One does not imply the others.

## Confirming the work basis

Before materially changing equipment, the engineer confirms the authorised basis of work. It may be:

- diagnosis only;
- planned-maintenance scope;
- accepted quoted repair;
- contract-covered minor repair;
- delegated spend limit;
- approved project task;
- suspected warranty investigation;
- authorised warranty remediation; or
- emergency make-safe action.

The engineer does not treat site presence, an informal request or a customer complaint as unlimited authority.

## Diagnosis

Fault finding proceeds from evidence:

1. confirm the reported condition and operational impact;
2. identify the correct system and asset;
3. observe the state on arrival;
4. review alarms, history, controls demand and operating conditions;
5. verify supplies, interlocks and prerequisites;
6. take relevant measurements under known conditions;
7. test hypotheses systematically;
8. distinguish root cause, contributing conditions and secondary symptoms; and
9. record confidence and remaining uncertainty.

A fault code identifies a detected condition, not necessarily the failed component. Speculative replacement is avoided.

## Customer descriptions and site statements

Customer statements are recorded as reported evidence, not automatically adopted as technical fact.

Examples include previous intermittent behaviour, recent building changes, another contractor's intervention or claims about what equipment serves which area.

The engineer listens respectfully while retaining responsibility for technical judgement.

## Authority to proceed

Where diagnosis identifies work beyond the current scope, the engineer separates:

- technical recommendation;
- customer authority;
- Frostline expenditure authority;
- availability of parts or resources; and
- decision to perform work now or create follow-on work.

Material verbal approval records the person, time, scope and limit. The engineer does not promise price, programme, warranty acceptance or replacement unless authorised to do so.

## Emergency and make-safe work

Emergency action may proceed where needed to control immediate risk. It should be limited to the reasonable make-safe or damage-prevention outcome.

The engineer records:

- condition encountered;
- immediate risk;
- action taken;
- equipment state left;
- customer informed;
- authority relied upon;
- cost or materials committed; and
- mandatory follow-up.

Make-safe is not restoration, permanent repair or job closure.

## Minor repairs

A minor repair may be completed where it is safe, technically justified, within competence, covered by existing scope or delegated authority and supported by available materials.

The record identifies why the engineer believed the work was authorised. “It was only a small part” is not an authority model.

## Temporary measures

Temporary repair, override, restricted operation or loan equipment creates a future obligation.

The engineer records:

- what was changed;
- reason;
- service restored;
- limitations and residual risk;
- monitoring requirement;
- expiry or review date;
- customer understanding;
- who approved it; and
- owner of permanent resolution.

The equipment must not be described simply as repaired where the underlying defect remains.

## Parts identification

Where parts are needed, the engineer provides enough evidence to support accurate procurement:

- manufacturer, model and serial;
- internal asset identifier;
- exact part number or identification basis;
- photographs and dimensions;
- electrical, pressure or capacity ratings;
- software or firmware relevance;
- quantity;
- associated seals, fittings or consumables;
- whether substitution is acceptable; and
- whether the removed item must be preserved for warranty or investigation.

“Needs a fan” is not a usable purchasing requirement.

## Unsafe or restricted systems

Where equipment cannot safely remain in normal operation, the engineer controls it within competence and authority through isolation, warning, restricted use or escalation.

The record states:

- hazard;
- control applied;
- residual risk;
- who was informed;
- whether the customer accepted custody of an isolated system; and
- next required action.

Customer objection to downtime does not authorise unsafe operation.

## Specific hazard responses

### Gas or combustion concern

The engineer follows applicable emergency and gas-safety procedures, protects occupants, isolates where competent and escalates to the appropriate competent person or emergency service.

### Electrical hazard

Exposed live parts, damaged supplies or unsafe isolation arrangements are controlled. Work outside the engineer's defined electrical competence is referred onward.

### Refrigerant leak

The engineer considers refrigerant type, concentration risk, ventilation, leak location, environmental duty and recovery requirements. Routine topping up without addressing a known leak is not accepted as permanent repair.

### Water escape or condensate risk

The engineer controls immediate damage where practical, identifies whether drainage, pump, installation, maintenance or external building conditions contributed, and records any areas requiring customer or third-party action.

## Testing and recommissioning

After intervention, the engineer tests enough to support the claimed outcome. Tests may include:

- pressure, vacuum or leak checks;
- electrical measurements;
- temperatures and pressures;
- control sequence and demand;
- alarms and safeties;
- drainage;
- airflow or water flow;
- combustion or emissions where applicable;
- configuration review; and
- observation through a suitable cycle or load condition.

The record states test conditions and limitations. A unit starting briefly does not prove durable repair or design performance.

## Attendance outcome

The engineer selects an attendance outcome that describes what happened, for example:

- authorised scope complete;
- partially complete;
- diagnosis complete, remedial work required;
- temporarily restored;
- made safe;
- no fault found under observed conditions;
- unable to complete because of access;
- unable to complete because of parts or tools;
- unable to complete because scope or authority changed;
- specialist review required; or
- customer cancelled after mobilisation.

The outcome applies to the attendance. The office or project owner determines the resulting job, warranty, invoice and customer-issue states.

## Photographs and evidence

Photographs may record identity, initial condition, readings, defects, completed work, removed parts, access and safety concerns.

Images are relevant, attributable and handled with regard to people, security information and customer confidentiality. They supplement written explanation rather than replacing it.

## Engineer notes

Useful notes distinguish:

- customer-reported symptom;
- observed facts;
- tests and conditions;
- working hypothesis;
- verified diagnosis;
- work performed;
- parts used or removed;
- final operating and safety condition;
- uncertainty;
- approvals and instructions;
- customer discussion;
- follow-on requirement; and
- urgency and next-action owner where known.

Notes should support technical continuation, commercial review, warranty assessment and customer communication.

## Customer handover

Before leaving, the engineer explains in proportionate language:

- what was found;
- what was done;
- current service and safety condition;
- whether the measure is temporary;
- any operating restriction;
- what remains unresolved;
- whether further work requires quotation or approval; and
- who will contact the customer next.

The engineer avoids definitive liability or warranty statements unless that decision has been authorised.

## Sign-off

Customer acknowledgement confirms attendance and receipt of the recorded outcome. It does not automatically:

- approve unquoted charges;
- accept technical design;
- waive defects;
- establish warranty responsibility; or
- confirm final job closure.

Where no authorised contact is available, alternative evidence and communication are recorded.

## Follow-on handoff

Where further action is required, the engineer provides a complete handoff containing:

- required outcome;
- technical evidence;
- exact dependency;
- asset safety and operating condition;
- parts or specialist requirement;
- customer commitments made;
- urgency;
- authority already obtained; and
- recommended next owner.

“Job incomplete” or “office to arrange” does not constitute a controlled handoff.

## Time, materials and expenses

Travel, attendance, labour, waiting, overtime, mileage, parking, tolls, materials and expenses are recorded accurately against the correct job, project or overhead purpose.

Time incurred is not automatically chargeable. Commercial treatment is decided from contract, scope, warranty and management evidence.

## Leaving site

Before departure, the engineer:

- leaves plant in a known safe state;
- reinstates guards and panels where appropriate;
- labels isolation or restrictions;
- removes tools and controlled waste;
- preserves required evidence;
- leaves the area orderly;
- returns keys and permits;
- signs out;
- confirms the customer has been updated; and
- submits records promptly.

A technically successful intervention with missing evidence, ambiguous status or no follow-on owner is not a professionally complete attendance.
