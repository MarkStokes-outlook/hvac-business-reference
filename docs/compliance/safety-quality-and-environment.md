# Safety, Quality and Environmental Controls

## Purpose

Frostline's work creates technical, safety, environmental and reputational risk. Controls must be proportionate to the work while remaining usable by engineers and managers under real operating pressure. This document uses the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md).

## Responsibility

The Operations Director holds executive responsibility for operational compliance. Managers are responsible for the work they plan and supervise. Every employee and subcontractor remains responsible for working safely, stopping where conditions are unsuitable and reporting concerns.

The Office and Compliance Coordinator maintains core records, supports renewals and gathers evidence. External advisers are used for specialist legal, health-and-safety and environmental matters.

## Risk assessment and method planning

Routine activities may use established assessments and methods where the actual conditions remain within their assumptions. Site-specific risks, unusual access, lifting, isolation, hot work, occupied premises or complex coordination require additional planning.

Documents are not treated as proof that work is safe. Engineers must confirm that the site matches the plan and reassess when conditions change.

## Site hazards

Common hazards include:

- electrical supplies and stored energy;
- pressurised refrigerant systems;
- hot work and ignition sources;
- working at height;
- roofs, fragile surfaces and weather exposure;
- lifting and movement of heavy equipment;
- asbestos-containing materials;
- confined or poorly ventilated spaces;
- occupied public areas;
- lone working;
- driving and roadside activity; and
- interaction with other contractors.

Work may be stopped or rescheduled where access, isolation, permits, weather, information or site control is inadequate.

## Refrigerants and environmental responsibility

Refrigerant handling is restricted to appropriately competent personnel. Frostline records refrigerant recovered, added, transferred or disposed of to the extent required by the work and applicable obligations.

Leaks are not treated merely as a reason to top up a system. Engineers assess cause, repairability, legal constraints, equipment condition and customer approval.

Recovered refrigerant, oils, filters, batteries, electrical equipment and other waste follow appropriate storage and disposal routes. Evidence from licensed waste or recycling providers is retained where required.

## Electrical and gas boundaries

Engineers work only within their competence and company authority. The boundary between HVAC diagnosis, electrical alteration and gas work must remain clear, especially where a fault crosses disciplines.

Specialist electrical or gas contractors are engaged where the activity falls outside Frostline's direct competence. Making equipment safe does not authorise a permanent repair beyond that competence.

## Tools, test equipment and access equipment

Tools and equipment must be suitable, maintained and inspected at the appropriate interval. Some test instruments require calibration or periodic verification.

Ladders, towers, lifting accessories, harnesses and other safety-critical equipment have identifiable inspection status. Damaged or overdue equipment is removed from use rather than left available in a van or store.

## Incident and near-miss management

Injuries, dangerous occurrences, environmental releases, vehicle incidents, property damage and significant near misses are reported promptly.

The immediate priority is safety and containment. Subsequent review establishes what happened, underlying causes, required notifications, corrective actions and whether similar work elsewhere is affected.

The purpose of review is learning and control, not automatic blame. Reckless conduct, concealment or deliberate breach is treated differently from an honest error in a weak system.

## Quality control

Quality means that work meets the agreed scope, technical requirements, manufacturer instructions and reasonable customer expectations.

Controls may include design review, material checks, supervisory visits, test results, commissioning records, photographs, peer support and post-completion review.

The level of independent checking depends on risk and complexity. A routine filter change does not need the same assurance as a large refrigerant installation or critical-system replacement.

## Defects and rework

Defects may be identified during installation, commissioning, customer inspection, later service or warranty response. Frostline records the issue, responsibility, corrective action and commercial treatment.

Not all repeat attendance is defective workmanship. Equipment failure, customer misuse, third-party interference, design change or unrelated faults may also cause return visits. The cause must be established before cost is allocated.

## Customer and site rules

Customer rules may add inductions, permits, security checks, safeguarding, hygiene controls, evidence standards or restricted working hours.

Frostline complies with valid site requirements but does not allow customer urgency to override legal duties or basic safety controls. Conflicts are escalated rather than informally accepted by an engineer under pressure.

## Subcontractor assurance

Subcontractors provide relevant competence, insurance and safety evidence before approval. Project-specific documents and inductions may still be required.

Frostline monitors actual performance, not only pre-qualification paperwork. Repeated poor records, unsafe behaviour, customer complaints or unauthorised substitution can lead to restricted or withdrawn approval.

## Records and retention

Compliance evidence includes qualifications, inspections, risk assessments, permits, waste records, refrigerant records, incident reports, commissioning evidence and corrective actions.

Retention varies by record type and contractual or legal need. Records must remain attributable and understandable; a photograph or unsigned form without context may have little evidential value.

## Current operating reality

Frostline has sound practical safety instincts and established compliance routines, but evidence is distributed across folders, inboxes, paper forms and specialist portals. Managers can usually reconstruct what happened, though doing so may depend on knowing where a particular person stores information.

The main risk is not an absence of concern. It is inconsistency between good field practice, formal evidence and the ability to prove that required controls were completed.
