# Business Metrics and KPIs

## Overview

Frostline uses business metrics to understand performance, identify risk and support decisions. Measures are useful only when their definitions are stable, their source data is credible and managers understand the behaviour they may encourage.

No single KPI defines a healthy HVAC business. Fast response can coexist with poor diagnosis, high utilisation can conceal overload and growing revenue can hide falling margin or weak cash collection. This document uses the record meanings in [Canonical terminology and record relationships](../governance/canonical-terminology-and-record-relationships.md).

## Measurement principles

A useful measure should have:

- a clear purpose;
- an agreed definition;
- a reliable source;
- a named owner;
- an appropriate reporting frequency;
- context or comparison; and
- an understood action when performance changes.

Frostline distinguishes between operational monitoring, management reporting and formal contractual reporting. The same measure may require different levels of detail for each audience.

## Leading and lagging indicators

Lagging indicators describe outcomes that have already occurred, such as revenue, gross margin, complaints or safety incidents.

Leading indicators provide earlier warning, such as overdue maintenance, open quotations, expiring certifications, repeated equipment alarms, rising overtime or increasing parts delays.

A balanced view uses both. Lagging measures confirm results; leading measures help change them.

## Operational KPIs

### Planned maintenance completion

The percentage of due planned visits completed within the agreed maintenance window. Reporting should distinguish customer cancellation, no access, Frostline capacity and formally rescheduled work.

### Reactive response

The proportion of reactive requests acknowledged or attended within the applicable target. Results are segmented by priority, contract and coverage period.

### First-time fix rate

The percentage of suitable reactive issues durably restored or resolved during the first attendance without an unplanned further engineering attendance.

It excludes cases where return work was always planned, where investigation was the authorised scope, or where no safe and justified repair could reasonably be expected. Frostline does not sacrifice diagnosis quality to improve the measure.

### Mean time to respond

Average elapsed time between the defined request point and the measured event. The metric must state which SLA event it measures — initial response and attendance are distinct events with different targets and should not be conflated in a single measure.

Averages are accompanied by percentiles or breach counts because a small number of long delays can be hidden.

### Mean time to repair

Average time from incident start to the measured outcome event. Restoration (return to agreed minimum service level) and resolution (completion of agreed permanent remedy) are distinct events and should be reported as separate measures. This is segmented where parts, customer approval or external dependencies materially affect elapsed time.

### Average job duration

Actual labour time compared with planned duration by job type. Persistent variance may indicate poor planning data, difficult assets, training needs or weak scope control.

### Engineer utilisation

The proportion of available paid time assigned to productive engineering or approved operational activity.

Utilisation excludes leave and other agreed unavailable time and is interpreted carefully. Very high utilisation can indicate insufficient travel allowance, missing administration time or no capacity for emergencies.

### Schedule reliability

The percentage of appointments attended within the promised window and the frequency of same-day schedule changes.

### Recall or callback rate

The proportion of operationally completed jobs requiring a return attendance because the original work was incomplete, incorrect or failed prematurely. Planned follow-on work and unrelated new faults are excluded.

### Open-work ageing

The number and value of jobs awaiting parts, approval, quotation, access, manufacturer advice or another action, grouped by age and owner.

## Commercial KPIs

### Quote turnaround

Elapsed working time from sufficient survey information to issue of a customer quotation. Results should separate simple service repairs from complex project estimates.

### Quote win rate

Accepted quotations as a proportion of decided quotations, measured by count and value. Open or withdrawn opportunities are reported separately.

Win rate is interpreted alongside margin and opportunity quality. Winning every quotation may indicate underpricing.

### Revenue per engineer

Recognised revenue related to engineering capacity over a defined period. This is a broad productivity indicator and not a direct measure of individual performance.

### Revenue per contract

Contract revenue by customer, site or asset base, used with service demand and direct cost to understand contract value.

### Contract renewal rate

The percentage and value of eligible service contracts renewed. Reasons for loss, reduction or expansion are recorded.

### Gross margin

Revenue less direct delivery cost, expressed as value and percentage. Direct-cost treatment is applied consistently across labour, materials, subcontractors and project expenses.

### Warranty cost

Labour, materials, travel and other cost absorbed because of workmanship, supplied equipment or unrecovered warranty attendance. Manufacturer recoveries are shown separately.

### Average invoice value

Average value of issued invoices by work type. It is useful for workload and transaction analysis but does not indicate profitability on its own.

## Financial KPIs

### Revenue and order intake

Revenue measures invoiced or otherwise recognised work; order intake measures newly secured commitments. The two should not be confused. The recognition point should be explicit: completion of attendance, achievement of project milestone, delivery of maintenance period or another defined trigger.

### Work in progress

The estimated value and cost of incomplete work, reviewed for accuracy and recoverability.

### Debtor days

The average time taken to collect customer debt. Frostline also monitors aged receivables, disputed invoices and concentration of overdue balances.

### Cash conversion

The movement from order to delivery, invoice and collected cash. Growth can increase cash pressure where materials and labour are paid before customers settle invoices.

### Stock turns

The rate at which held stock is consumed or sold. Low movement may indicate excessive, obsolete or poorly located inventory.

### Parts availability

The proportion of required parts available when needed, balanced against the cost of holding stock.

## Customer KPIs

### Customer satisfaction

Feedback collected after service, projects or periodic account reviews. Method and response volume are stated so that small samples are not overstated.

### Net Promoter Score

Where used, NPS is calculated consistently and treated as one signal rather than a complete account-health measure.

### Complaint rate

Formal complaints by job, customer or revenue level, with category, severity, root cause and time to complaint closure. Complaint closure is distinct from the SLA resolution event; it requires customer acknowledgement and completion of any corrective or goodwill actions.

Low complaint volume is not automatically positive if customers have stopped reporting concerns or moved business elsewhere.

### Communication compliance

The proportion of significant open incidents updated at the promised interval. Customers often judge uncertainty and silence more harshly than a clearly explained delay.

## Engineering and quality KPIs

Engineering measures may include:

- diagnostic accuracy;
- callback rate;
- commissioning defects;
- incomplete documentation;
- equipment damage;
- recurring fault patterns;
- training completion; and
- supervisor quality findings.

Measures are normally used to identify team and process needs before being used for individual comparison.

## Compliance KPIs

Frostline monitors:

- certification expiry;
- calibration compliance;
- vehicle inspection and servicing;
- planned statutory checks;
- risk-assessment completion;
- permit or evidence completeness;
- waste and refrigerant records;
- training compliance;
- accidents and near misses; and
- corrective-action closure.

A high form-completion rate does not prove that controls are effective. Sampling and quality review remain necessary.

## Health and safety measures

Safety reporting includes incidents, near misses, lost time, vehicle accidents, unsafe-condition reports and corrective actions.

Near-miss reporting is encouraged. An apparent increase can indicate better reporting rather than worsening safety, so context matters.

## Dashboards

Operational dashboards focus on current action, such as today's schedule, urgent incidents, overdue visits, unavailable engineers, parts dependencies and aged open jobs.

Dashboards should make exceptions visible without overwhelming users with every available data point.

## Weekly management reporting

Weekly reviews typically cover:

- service demand and SLA risk;
- maintenance backlog;
- engineering capacity;
- open quotations;
- significant customer issues;
- parts and subcontract dependencies;
- invoicing blockers;
- safety events; and
- actions requiring management decision.

The purpose is active control rather than production of attractive charts.

## Monthly board reporting

Monthly reporting provides a wider view of:

- revenue, margin and cash;
- order intake and pipeline;
- contract performance and renewal;
- operational capacity;
- customer concentration;
- quality and safety;
- people, training and absence;
- fleet and asset risk; and
- material opportunities or threats.

Narrative explains significant variance, causes, corrective action and expected outlook.

## Seasonal and trend analysis

HVAC performance is affected by weather and season. Frostline compares results with prior periods, expected seasonal patterns and relevant workload drivers.

A hot summer may improve cooling revenue while worsening response performance. Both effects should be visible.

## Benchmarking

External or historical benchmarks can help but require comparable definitions, business mix and geography. Frostline does not treat an industry headline as a target without understanding how it was calculated.

## Data quality

Common data-quality problems include:

- inconsistent job closure;
- missing labour or materials;
- duplicate customer or asset records;
- inaccurate priority codes;
- open jobs without next actions;
- delayed invoice status;
- changed KPI definitions; and
- manual adjustments without explanation.

Poor data is identified rather than silently converted into confident reporting.

## Ownership and action

Each important KPI has an owner responsible for definition, review and follow-up. Ownership does not mean that one person controls every underlying cause.

Where a measure moves outside an acceptable range, the response is to understand the cause, agree action and review whether the measure itself remains useful. KPIs are decision tools, not decorative scorekeeping.
