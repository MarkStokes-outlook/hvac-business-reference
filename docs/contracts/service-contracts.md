# Service Contracts

## Overview

Service contracts provide customers with an agreed framework for planned maintenance, reactive support and continuing care of their HVAC assets. They give Frostline visibility of future workload and recurring revenue while giving customers clearer service expectations, maintenance records and access to engineering support.

A contract does not remove uncertainty from engineering work. It defines responsibilities, scope, priorities and commercial treatment so that routine questions do not have to be renegotiated every time a fault occurs. This document uses the record meanings in [Canonical terminology and record relationships](../governance/canonical-terminology-and-record-relationships.md) and the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md). The SLA event chain, priority model and clock-stop rules are defined in [SLAs, response and coverage](slas-response-and-coverage.md).

## Contract types

Frostline uses several broad contract forms:

- planned maintenance only;
- planned maintenance with preferential reactive response;
- planned maintenance with labour included for defined reactive work;
- comprehensive cover including specified labour and parts;
- seasonal or limited-period cover;
- multi-site framework agreements; and
- bespoke agreements for critical or unusual plant.

The contract title is less important than the written scope. Terms such as "fully comprehensive" can create false expectations unless included equipment, labour, parts, exclusions and limits are explicit.

## Domestic and commercial arrangements

Most Frostline contracts are commercial. Commercial customers may have multiple sites, formal purchase-order rules, site-specific access procedures, agreed response targets and reporting requirements.

Domestic plans are usually simpler and may focus on annual servicing, safety checks and discounted or preferential reactive attendance. Consumer-facing terms must be clear and proportionate, particularly around cancellation, renewal and exclusions.

## Contract lifecycle

A service contract normally passes through the following stages:

1. opportunity identification;
2. site survey and asset verification;
3. scope and maintenance-plan design;
4. pricing and internal approval;
5. proposal and negotiation;
6. customer acceptance;
7. mobilisation;
8. ongoing delivery and review;
9. renewal, variation or expiry; and
10. demobilisation where the contract ends.

Weak mobilisation creates problems that continue for the life of the agreement. Asset lists, site contacts, access requirements, invoicing rules, maintenance dates and escalation routes should be confirmed before the first planned visit.

## Asset schedules

The asset schedule identifies the equipment covered by the agreement. It may record:

- site and location;
- asset identifier;
- equipment type;
- manufacturer and model;
- serial number;
- duty or served area;
- refrigerant where applicable;
- maintenance frequency;
- known condition; and
- specific exclusions or limitations.

An asset not listed is not automatically covered merely because it is physically present at the site. Equally, Frostline should not knowingly continue charging for equipment that has been removed or permanently decommissioned.

Asset schedules are reviewed when equipment is added, replaced, relocated or removed. Material changes may require a contract variation and repricing.

## Planned maintenance scope

The contract defines the number and frequency of planned visits and the expected service activities. Frequencies may vary by asset type, usage, environmental conditions, statutory requirement and manufacturer recommendation.

A maintenance visit normally includes inspection, routine cleaning, testing, operating checks, recorded readings and identification of defects. It does not automatically include remedial works, replacement parts or restoration of equipment already in poor condition unless the contract says otherwise.

## Reactive support

Reactive entitlement may include:

- access to Frostline's service desk;
- priority over non-contracted customers;
- agreed response targets;
- out-of-hours call handling;
- included labour within defined periods;
- discounted labour rates; or
- emergency attendance arrangements.

Reactive coverage should distinguish between acknowledgement, remote support, attendance, temporary restoration and completed repair. A response target is not a guarantee that a fault can be permanently repaired within the same period.

## Included labour

Where labour is included, the agreement states which activities and working periods qualify. Included labour may be limited to normal working hours, reactive diagnosis, routine adjustment or repairs to listed assets.

Labour may remain chargeable where work results from:

- misuse or accidental damage;
- third-party interference;
- building or electrical faults outside Frostline's scope;
- unauthorised alterations;
- failure to follow operating advice;
- excluded components;
- access equipment not included in the agreement; or
- work requested outside covered hours.

## Parts and consumables

Contracts distinguish between routine consumables, minor service materials and replacement parts.

Items such as cleaning materials, lubricants, standard fixings and selected filters may be included. Compressors, motors, controls, heat exchangers and other significant components are normally excluded unless comprehensive cover specifically includes them.

Even where parts are included, obsolete, unavailable or specialist items may require separate treatment. Frostline does not promise availability that manufacturers or suppliers cannot provide.

## Exclusions

Typical exclusions include:

- pre-existing defects;
- equipment not on the asset schedule;
- damage caused by flood, fire, vandalism or pests;
- loss of utilities or upstream services;
- fabric, drainage, structural or builders' work;
- specialist access, cranes or scaffolding;
- asbestos-related work;
- refrigerant losses caused by third-party damage;
- consequential loss;
- statutory upgrades not foreseeable at contract start; and
- replacement of equipment beyond economic repair.

Exclusions are not a device for avoiding legitimate responsibility. They should describe risks that are genuinely outside the priced service.

## Customer obligations

Customers are normally responsible for:

- providing safe and timely access;
- identifying site hazards;
- maintaining clear access to equipment;
- supplying permits, inductions and escorts where needed;
- operating equipment reasonably;
- reporting faults promptly;
- preventing unauthorised interference;
- providing purchase orders or approvals without avoidable delay; and
- paying invoices in accordance with agreed terms.

Customer-caused delay may affect response or completion performance.

## Frostline obligations

Frostline is responsible for delivering the agreed services with competent personnel, suitable records and reasonable care. It maintains appropriate certifications, insurance, tools and management controls for the work undertaken.

Frostline also communicates material findings, safety concerns, delays, outstanding defects and actions requiring customer approval.

## Multi-site contracts

Multi-site agreements may contain a common commercial framework with site-specific schedules. Different sites can have different operating hours, asset mixes, response priorities and access rules.

Performance should be considered both across the portfolio and at individual sites. Strong overall statistics should not conceal repeated failure at one location.

## Contract pricing

Pricing may be based on:

- number and type of assets;
- planned labour hours;
- travel and geography;
- visit frequency;
- reactive entitlement;
- out-of-hours exposure;
- included parts or consumables;
- access requirements;
- reporting obligations;
- condition and age of equipment; and
- risk and management overhead.

Low initial pricing can create a contract that is impossible to serve properly. Frostline reviews both revenue and actual delivery cost when assessing contract performance.

## Review and renewal

Contracts are reviewed before renewal. The review considers:

- asset changes;
- maintenance completion;
- reactive demand;
- parts and subcontract cost;
- response performance;
- recurring defects;
- customer feedback;
- equipment condition;
- price adequacy; and
- any changes in law, standards or manufacturer requirements.

Renewal is not purely an inflation exercise. A contract may need redesign where the service pattern or risk has materially changed.

## Variations

A contract variation records an agreed change in scope, price, term or service level. Verbal requests should not quietly become permanent obligations.

Common variations include adding assets, changing visit frequency, extending coverage hours, adding sites or revising reporting requirements.

## Suspension and termination

Service may be suspended or restricted for serious non-payment, unsafe access, abuse of personnel or other material breach, subject to contractual and legal requirements.

Termination arrangements should address notice, outstanding work, final invoicing, return of customer property, handover of records and removal of Frostline stock or equipment from site.

## Disputes and ambiguity

Common contract disputes arise from:

- assumptions that all repairs are included;
- incomplete asset schedules;
- disagreement over pre-existing defects;
- confusion between response and resolution;
- delayed customer approvals;
- parts obsolescence;
- unrecorded verbal promises; and
- automatic renewal or cancellation expectations.

Clear records, consistent communication and fair interpretation prevent more disputes than aggressive contract wording.
