# Vans, Tools and Calibration

## Overview

Frostline's fleet and engineering equipment enable safe, reliable field work. Vehicles are mobile workplaces, stores and company assets. Tools and instruments must be suitable, available, maintained and traceable where their condition affects safety or technical evidence.

Poor control of vehicles and equipment creates direct cost, lost engineer time, failed visits and avoidable risk. This document uses the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md).

## Fleet composition

The fleet mainly consists of service and installation vans, with vehicle size and specification matched to role. Some engineers require larger load capacity, racking or specialist equipment, while others carry a lighter service stock.

Vehicle allocation considers:

- engineer role;
- expected mileage;
- load and towing requirements;
- security;
- emissions or clean-air restrictions;
- access to customer sites; and
- home-parking or charging arrangements.

## Vehicle allocation

A vehicle may be allocated to an individual engineer or shared within a team. The assigned driver remains responsible for routine checks, cleanliness, defect reporting and reasonable security during their period of use.

Allocation does not transfer ownership of stock or tools to the driver.

## Daily and periodic checks

Drivers check items such as tyres, lights, fluids, warning indicators, mirrors, load security and visible damage. Defects are reported promptly and vehicles are not used where their condition would be unsafe or unlawful.

Periodic inspections, servicing, MOT testing and manufacturer recalls are centrally tracked. A diary reminder alone is insufficient where bookings, downtime and replacement vehicles must be planned.

## Breakdowns

Breakdown arrangements identify who to contact, how the vehicle and load are secured and how urgent customer commitments are reassigned.

The engineer keeps the coordinator informed. A broken-down vehicle can affect several appointments and may expose tools, refrigerant or customer equipment that require secure recovery.

## Accidents and damage

Road incidents are reported promptly with appropriate evidence, third-party details and internal escalation. Engineers do not admit liability on behalf of Frostline at the roadside.

Minor damage is still recorded. Repeated unexplained damage may indicate training, parking or workload problems.

## Fuel and charging

Fuel cards are used only for authorised business purposes and supported by accurate vehicle mileage. Exceptions and unusual transactions are reviewed.

Where electric vehicles are used, charging access, range, payload effect, winter performance and route suitability are considered. Charging time is an operational dependency, not merely a payroll or driver issue.

## Vehicle cleanliness and presentation

Vans should be reasonably clean, organised and suitable for arrival at customer premises. External branding, number plates and safety markings must remain legible.

The interior should allow safe loading and quick identification of stock. A van full of unclassified parts and waste is not an effective store.

## Load security

Tools, cylinders, equipment and materials are secured against movement. Payload and axle limits are respected.

Gas and refrigerant cylinders are transported, stored and labelled in accordance with applicable requirements. They are not left loose or exposed to avoidable heat and damage.

## Van stock

Standard van stock supports common service and repair activity. Stock profiles may differ by engineering discipline and vehicle role.

Control includes:

- defined standard quantities;
- issue and replenishment;
- periodic counts;
- shelf-life or condition checks;
- transfer between engineers;
- return of unused project materials; and
- identification of obsolete or damaged items.

Engineers should not hoard scarce parts for hypothetical future use.

## Consumables

Consumables include fixings, sealants, cleaning products, electrical items, filters, brazing materials and other frequently used supplies. Minimum quantities are balanced against vehicle space, weight, cost and expiry.

## Tool ownership

Tools may be company-owned, individually issued or, by explicit agreement, personally owned. Ownership, maintenance responsibility and permitted use should be clear.

Frostline does not rely on engineers to provide unspecified personal tools that are essential to normal work.

## Tool issue and return

Significant tools and instruments are recorded when issued. Records may include asset number, serial number, assigned user, condition and accessories.

On role change or departure, issued equipment is returned and checked. Missing items are investigated fairly rather than assumed to be theft or written off without review.

## Inspection and maintenance

Hand tools, power tools, ladders, lifting aids and safety equipment are inspected at suitable intervals and after damage or abnormal use. Defective equipment is removed from service and clearly quarantined.

Temporary tape or an engineer's memory is not an acceptable long-term control for a known defect.

## Test instruments

Typical engineering instruments include:

- electrical meters and clamp meters;
- insulation or continuity testers;
- refrigerant gauges and probes;
- vacuum gauges;
- pressure instruments;
- thermometers;
- airflow instruments;
- combustion analysers;
- leak detectors; and
- data loggers.

Selection depends on the work undertaken and the accuracy required.

## Calibration

Calibration provides confidence that an instrument's readings are acceptably related to recognised standards. Not every tool requires formal calibration, but instruments used for safety, commissioning, compliance or technical evidence may require controlled verification.

Calibration records may include:

- asset and serial number;
- calibration date;
- result and certificate;
- due date;
- range or limitations;
- repair or adjustment; and
- assigned engineer.

An in-date label does not replace the certificate or condition check.

## Out-of-calibration equipment

Where an instrument is found outside acceptable tolerance, Frostline considers whether previous work or recorded results may have been affected. The response depends on the size of error, work performed and safety significance.

The instrument is removed from use until adjusted, repaired or replaced.

## Gas analysers and specialist instruments

Combustion analysers, leak detectors and similar instruments may require regular calibration, sensor replacement, charging, clean filters and controlled storage.

Engineers perform pre-use checks and do not assume that formal calibration guarantees correct operation after contamination or damage.

## Electrical equipment safety

Portable electrical equipment is maintained through risk-based inspection and testing. Formal portable-appliance testing may form part of the control, but user checks and defect reporting remain essential.

Chargers, extension leads and site transformers receive the same attention as major power tools.

## PPE

PPE issue is based on risk and role. Typical items include safety footwear, gloves, eye protection, hearing protection, high-visibility clothing, respiratory protection and task-specific equipment.

PPE is inspected, replaced when worn and stored to avoid damage. Disposable items are replenished before the engineer reaches site.

## Security and theft prevention

Vehicles should be locked, tools concealed where practical and high-value equipment removed where risk is elevated. Engineers follow local rules for overnight parking and key control.

Theft is reported promptly to management and police where appropriate, with lists of missing assets and customer property.

Vehicle tracking or security systems are used proportionately and transparently. Their purpose is operational safety and asset protection, not casual surveillance.

## Lost or damaged equipment

Loss and damage are recorded with the circumstances, operational effect and replacement need. The aim is to restore capability and identify preventable patterns.

Normal wear, accidental damage, negligence and deliberate misuse are not treated as the same event.

## Replacement planning

Fleet and tool replacement considers age, reliability, maintenance cost, downtime, safety, emissions, supportability and changing work requirements.

Keeping an asset until catastrophic failure may appear economical while creating higher total cost through breakdowns and lost work.

## Performance and review

Useful measures include fleet availability, maintenance cost, fuel or energy use, accidents, vehicle downtime, tool loss, calibration compliance, stock variance and failed visits caused by missing equipment.

Measures should drive practical improvement rather than punish honest defect reporting.
