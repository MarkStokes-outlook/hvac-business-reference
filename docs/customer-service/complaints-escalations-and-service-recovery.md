# Complaints, Escalations and Service Recovery

## Overview

Complaints are treated as operational evidence, not merely difficult conversations. They may reveal service failure, poor communication, unsafe work, commercial misunderstanding, recurring equipment problems or unrealistic customer expectations.

The immediate objective is to understand the concern, protect people and property, stabilise the relationship and determine a fair next action. This document uses the record meanings in [Canonical terminology and record relationships](../governance/canonical-terminology-and-record-relationships.md) and the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md). Exception routing follows [Exceptions, handoffs and next-action control](../operations/exceptions-handoffs-and-next-action-control.md).

## What counts as a complaint

A complaint is any clear expression that Frostline's service, conduct, work, communication or commercial treatment was unacceptable. It does not need to use the word "complaint" or arrive through a formal channel.

Common subjects include:

- missed or late attendance;
- failure to provide updates;
- repeat breakdowns;
- disputed diagnosis or workmanship;
- damage or cleanliness concerns;
- engineer behaviour;
- unexpected charges;
- invoice discrepancies;
- failure to meet a contractual commitment; and
- dissatisfaction with the proposed remedy.

## Initial response

The first recipient should listen, acknowledge the concern and capture the facts without becoming defensive or admitting liability prematurely.

Immediate questions include:

- Is anyone at risk?
- Is equipment unsafe or causing damage?
- Is the site without a critical service?
- Does evidence need to be preserved?
- Is urgent attendance required?
- Who owns the next communication?

A prompt acknowledgement is valuable even when investigation will take longer.

## Complaint ownership

Each complaint has a named owner responsible for coordination and communication. The owner may be a service manager, project manager, commercial manager or director depending on severity and subject.

Ownership does not mean conducting every investigation personally. It means ensuring that evidence is gathered, actions are assigned and the customer is not left to chase several people.

## Investigation

Relevant evidence may include:

- job instructions and engineer notes;
- photographs and readings;
- call recordings or correspondence;
- quotation scope and exclusions;
- attendance and vehicle records;
- asset history;
- parts supplied;
- commissioning or test results;
- witness accounts; and
- subcontractor records.

The investigation should distinguish verified facts, professional opinion and unresolved claims.

## Severity and escalation

Higher-level escalation is appropriate where there is:

- actual or potential injury;
- gas, electrical, pressure or refrigerant safety risk;
- significant property damage;
- allegation of dishonesty, discrimination or serious misconduct;
- regulatory involvement;
- likely legal action;
- substantial financial exposure;
- major customer relationship risk; or
- evidence of a repeated systemic failure.

Insurers, legal advisers, manufacturers or regulators may need to be notified. Notifications should not be delayed merely to complete an internal investigation.

## Service recovery

Possible remedies include:

- explanation or apology;
- urgent reattendance;
- corrective work;
- independent inspection;
- replacement parts or equipment;
- invoice correction;
- credit or refund;
- contribution toward reasonable loss;
- revised operating guidance; and
- termination of the relationship where trust cannot be restored.

The remedy should address the actual failure. A small credit is not an adequate substitute for unresolved unsafe workmanship, and repeated free visits may not solve a fundamentally unsuitable system.

## Liability and goodwill

A goodwill gesture may be offered without accepting legal liability. Conversely, Frostline should not avoid responsibility where evidence shows that its work caused the problem.

Commercial settlements require suitable authority and a clear record of what has been agreed. Confidentiality or full-and-final-settlement terms are used only where appropriate and properly reviewed.

## Customer contribution

Some complaints involve shared causes, such as poor access, unauthorised alterations, ignored recommendations, misuse, delayed approval or equipment outside Frostline's scope.

These factors should be explained respectfully and supported by evidence. Blaming the customer as a reflex is poor service; ignoring material customer actions is equally unhelpful.

## Communication

Customers should receive realistic update dates. An update may state that the investigation remains open, but it should still explain what has been completed, what remains outstanding and when the next contact will occur.

Technical explanations should be understandable without oversimplifying the facts. Internal disagreement should be resolved before contradictory explanations are given.

## Closure

A complaint is closed when:

- the findings have been communicated;
- agreed corrective actions are complete or scheduled;
- financial adjustments are authorised;
- any safety or compliance actions are complete;
- the customer's response is recorded; and
- learning actions have owners.

A customer does not have to agree with the outcome for the complaint to be closed, but Frostline must be able to explain the basis of its decision.

## Learning and prevention

Complaints are reviewed for patterns including poor handovers, weak diagnosis, inadequate notes, recurring supplier faults, unrealistic scheduling and unclear quotation wording.

The useful measure is not simply complaint count. Low recorded complaints can mean excellent service, or it can mean complaints are being hidden, discouraged or handled informally without learning.
