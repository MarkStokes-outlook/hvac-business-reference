# Estimating and Quotation

## Overview

Estimating converts an identified customer need into a technically credible and commercially sustainable offer. A good quotation explains what Frostline proposes to do, what the price includes, what assumptions have been made and what remains outside scope.

The lowest price is not automatically the best estimate. Underpricing creates delivery pressure, disputes and poor-quality decisions; overpricing without explanation loses otherwise suitable work.

This document uses the canonical meanings of enquiry, opportunity, estimate, quotation, variation, cost, price, margin, job and project defined in [Canonical terminology and record relationships](../governance/canonical-terminology-and-record-relationships.md). Authority to approve scope, price, discounts, risk and company expenditure is governed by [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md). Handover from estimating to operations follows the ownership-transfer rules in [Exceptions, handoffs and next-action control](../operations/exceptions-handoffs-and-next-action-control.md).

## Opportunity qualification

Before investing significant estimating effort, Frostline considers:

- customer fit and payment history;
- location and delivery capacity;
- technical competence required;
- timescale and access constraints;
- likelihood of award;
- tender requirements;
- project value and risk; and
- whether the opportunity matches Frostline's services.

Some enquiries are declined where the scope is unsuitable, information is inadequate or the requested programme cannot be responsibly supported.

Qualification is a decision to pursue, not a promise to quote and not customer authority to begin work. The opportunity record identifies its owner, next action, decision date and any information still required.

## Estimate maturity

Frostline distinguishes between different levels of cost and price confidence:

- **budget indication** — an early range used for feasibility or customer planning;
- **concept estimate** — based on a proposed solution but still subject to survey, design or supplier confirmation;
- **developed estimate** — supported by a sufficiently defined scope, measured quantities, labour plan and current supplier evidence;
- **approved quotation basis** — internally reviewed and suitable to issue as a formal offer; and
- **delivery forecast** — the current expected cost after award, incorporating approved changes and known delivery information.

These are related records but not interchangeable. A budget indication must not silently become a fixed-price commitment, and a delivery forecast must not rewrite the accepted quotation history.

## Site survey

The estimator gathers enough information to understand existing conditions. A survey may record:

- site layout and access;
- existing plant and controls;
- model and serial details;
- served areas and operating requirements;
- electrical supplies and isolators;
- pipework, ductwork and drainage routes;
- lifting and working-at-height requirements;
- fire stopping and builders' work;
- asbestos information;
- working-hour restrictions;
- noise or planning constraints; and
- commissioning and handover expectations.

Photographs, sketches and measurements support the written survey but do not replace clear notes.

Where information remains unverified, the estimate records the uncertainty and how it is treated: further survey, customer dependency, provisional allowance, exclusion, option or explicit risk acceptance. “Site to be confirmed” is not a sufficient control for a material unknown.

## Existing system assessment

Replacement and alteration work requires an assessment of the existing system. Frostline considers condition, capacity, compatibility, controls, remaining life and known defects.

Existing equipment may have been modified since installation. Drawings and labels are treated as useful evidence, not unquestionable truth.

The quotation distinguishes observed facts, customer-provided information, design assumptions and professional recommendations. This prevents uncertain evidence from later appearing to have been guaranteed.

## Design and equipment selection

Equipment selection considers duty, load, diversity, occupancy, building use, external conditions, efficiency, refrigerant, electrical demand, controls integration, noise, maintenance access and manufacturer support.

For heating work, heat-loss information and design temperatures may be required. For cooling and ventilation, internal gains, fresh-air requirements and operating patterns may materially affect selection.

Frostline avoids deliberately undersizing equipment to win work or oversizing it merely to create apparent safety margin.

Where design responsibility is shared, the quotation states who is responsible for calculations, selections, coordination, approvals and information supplied by others. Reviewing another party's design does not automatically transfer full design responsibility to Frostline.

## Labour estimating

Labour estimates include more than installation time. They may cover:

- mobilisation and unloading;
- isolations and permits;
- removal of existing equipment;
- installation and connection;
- pressure testing, evacuation or flushing;
- commissioning;
- controls setup;
- documentation and demonstration;
- supervision;
- travel and overnight requirements; and
- realistic allowance for restricted access or phased work.

Complex work may require multiple engineers, specialist grades or attendance from another trade.

Labour assumptions identify crew composition, normal working hours, number of mobilisations and dependencies on other parties. Productivity allowances should reflect the actual site rather than an ideal installation performed without interruption.

## Materials and bought-in services

Material estimates may include equipment, pipework, ductwork, insulation, controls, electrical components, fixings, supports, consumables, refrigerant, filters and disposal materials.

Bought-in services may include:

- crane or lifting operations;
- scaffolding or powered access;
- electrical work;
- roofing or builders' work;
- asbestos surveys or removal;
- fire stopping;
- specialist commissioning;
- controls integration; and
- waste disposal.

Supplier prices, freight, lead times and validity dates are checked where they could affect the offer.

A supplier quotation is evidence of likely cost, not authority to purchase. Frostline purchase commitments follow the separate approval and purchasing controls defined in the governance and supply-chain references.

## Risk and contingency

Risk allowances reflect identifiable uncertainty rather than hidden profit. Relevant risks include poor records, concealed services, fragile existing equipment, difficult access, uncertain ground or roof conditions, phased working and volatile supplier pricing.

Contingency should be proportionate and subject to internal judgement. It does not excuse an incomplete survey.

Material risks are recorded with an owner and treatment. The treatment may be to avoid, reduce, transfer, price, qualify or consciously accept the risk. A contingency value without an explanation provides weak handover evidence and is easily consumed by unrelated cost.

## Cost, margin and price

The estimate brings together direct labour, materials, subcontract cost, plant hire, travel, project management, overhead contribution, risk and profit margin.

Margin is not the same as mark-up. Internal review checks that the intended gross margin remains credible after foreseeable delivery cost.

VAT treatment is stated correctly and consistently.

The cost model, proposed price and approval decision remain distinguishable. A reviewer may approve issuing a quotation at a particular price without asserting that every underlying cost is certain.

## Assumptions

Assumptions make the basis of price visible. Examples include:

- work during normal hours;
- unrestricted access to agreed areas;
- suitable existing electrical supply;
- structurally adequate mounting surfaces;
- no asbestos disturbance;
- customer-provided shutdown approval;
- clear routes for pipework or ductwork; and
- use of specified equipment remaining available.

An assumption should be realistic and relevant. It should not quietly transfer every foreseeable risk to the customer.

Each material assumption should be testable. Where failure of an assumption would change price, programme or method, the quotation states the consequence or variation route rather than leaving it implicit.

## Exclusions

Exclusions identify work not included, such as decorating, structural alterations, utility upgrades, planning applications, specialist surveys or removal of undisclosed hazardous materials.

Exclusions must not contradict the main scope.

An exclusion does not remove Frostline's duty to coordinate known interfaces or warn about foreseeable consequences. It defines responsibility; it is not permission to ignore the surrounding system.

## Options and alternatives

Where useful, Frostline may present:

- a compliant base solution;
- higher-efficiency equipment;
- enhanced controls;
- alternative manufacturers;
- phased replacement;
- maintenance options; or
- separately priced remedial work.

Options should be genuinely comparable and clearly identify any change in performance or scope.

The customer's acceptance must identify which option or combination is authorised. Acceptance of one option does not authorise omitted enhancements or assumptions from another.

## Quotation content

A formal quotation normally includes:

- customer and site;
- quotation number and revision;
- scope of works;
- equipment and principal materials;
- programme or lead-time statement;
- price and VAT;
- payment terms;
- validity period;
- assumptions and exclusions;
- warranty position;
- customer responsibilities; and
- acceptance method.

Where relevant it also identifies key systems and assets, expected outage, design responsibility, provisional sums, options, required access, dependencies, deliverables and the boundary between making safe, temporary restoration and permanent completion.

A provisional sum within an otherwise fixed-price quotation means the stated value is an allowance subject to adjustment once the identified uncertainty is resolved. The quotation states what triggers the adjustment, how the final value will be determined and whether customer approval is required before the provisional element proceeds. A provisional sum is not an invitation for Frostline to incur any cost and recover it later without agreement.

## Internal approval

Higher-value, lower-margin or higher-risk quotations receive additional review. Approval may consider technical solution, supplier evidence, labour allowance, contractual risk, cash flow, warranty exposure and delivery capacity.

Internal approval should identify:

- the quotation revision approved;
- the proposed price and expected margin;
- material qualifications or departures;
- approval conditions;
- delegated authority relied upon; and
- any matter requiring director, finance, technical or legal review.

Approval to issue is not approval to purchase equipment or commence delivery. Those commitments arise only after valid customer authority, credit acceptance where required and operational release.

## Revision control

Changed quotations retain a clear revision history. Revisions identify what changed and supersede earlier versions where appropriate.

Verbal discussion is recorded when it affects price, scope or programme. Frostline does not rely on memory to distinguish the accepted offer from an earlier draft.

A revised quotation does not automatically cancel an already accepted contract. Where acceptance has occurred, subsequent changes are controlled as clarifications, amendments or variations according to their legal and commercial effect.

## Acceptance validation

Before treating a quotation as accepted, Frostline checks:

- the accepting party is identifiable and has suitable authority;
- the accepted quotation number and revision are clear;
- selected options are unambiguous;
- the purchase order or acceptance value matches the offer;
- customer terms do not introduce unreviewed conditions;
- requested dates are still achievable;
- quotation validity and supplier pricing remain usable;
- credit or deposit requirements are satisfied or explicitly waived; and
- qualifications, assumptions and exclusions have not been contradicted.

A purchase order with a familiar number is not sufficient if its scope, value, legal entity or terms do not match the quotation.

## Commercial release to delivery

Award creates a controlled transition from opportunity to authorised work. Frostline records:

- the customer authority relied upon;
- the internally approved quotation revision;
- the job or project created;
- budget ownership;
- required deposits or credit conditions;
- permitted purchasing commitments;
- promised milestones;
- unresolved dependencies; and
- the person owning the next action.

Where acceptance is incomplete or conditional, the work remains awaiting commercial release rather than being described vaguely as “won” or “live”.

## Handover after award

The estimator hands the accepted basis to operations or project delivery. The handover includes surveys, correspondence, supplier quotations, assumptions, exclusions, programme commitments and commercial risks.

A signed customer order does not repair a weak internal handover.

The receiving owner reviews the handover rather than merely receiving files. Any contradiction between accepted scope, estimate, delivery plan or customer expectation is raised before irreversible commitments are made.

## Changes after award

New information after award is classified before action:

- clarification within the accepted scope;
- correction of Frostline's own error;
- customer-requested change;
- site condition differing from an explicit assumption;
- omission or dependency belonging to another party;
- emergency make-safe work; or
- separately proposed follow-on work.

The classification affects technical response, customer communication, cost responsibility and whether a variation is required. Discovery alone does not constitute approval.

## Quote analysis

Frostline reviews quotation performance through measures such as turnaround time, win rate, gross margin, value by service type and reasons for loss.

Loss reasons are interpreted carefully. "Too expensive" may mean genuine price difference, unsuitable scope, weak customer relationship or a competitor misunderstanding the work.

Post-delivery review compares the accepted estimate basis with actual labour, materials, subcontract cost, changes and margin. The purpose is to improve future decisions, not to rewrite the original estimate using hindsight.
