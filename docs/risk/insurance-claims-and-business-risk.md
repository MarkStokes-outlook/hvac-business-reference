# Insurance, Claims and Business Risk

## Overview

Frostline manages operational risk through competent work, clear contracts, supervision, maintenance of vehicles and tools, financial control and appropriate insurance. Insurance is a backstop for defined losses; it is not a substitute for good management. This document uses the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md).

## Main risk areas

Business risks include:

- injury to employees, customers or the public;
- damage to customer property;
- defective workmanship;
- design error;
- refrigerant or environmental release;
- vehicle collision;
- theft of tools, stock or equipment;
- cyber or data incident;
- business interruption;
- employee dishonesty;
- contractual penalties;
- bad debt; and
- dependency on key people, suppliers or customers.

Risks may overlap. A single plant-room incident can involve safety, property damage, service interruption, regulatory notification and reputational harm.

## Insurance arrangements

Relevant covers may include employers' liability, public liability, products liability, professional indemnity where design responsibility exists, motor insurance, property and stock cover, tools in transit, business interruption and cyber cover.

Policy scope, exclusions, excesses, territorial limits and notification requirements are reviewed. A certificate showing a large headline limit does not prove that every activity is covered.

## Contract review

Customer contracts may require particular insurance limits or attempt to transfer broad liabilities to Frostline.

Before acceptance, Frostline considers whether obligations are insurable, proportionate and consistent with the quoted price. Unlimited liability, responsibility for indirect loss or acceptance of design duties can materially change the risk.

## Incident response

Following an incident, priorities are:

1. protect life and prevent further harm;
2. make the site safe within competence and authority;
3. contact emergency services or specialists where required;
4. preserve evidence;
5. notify management;
6. meet regulatory or contractual reporting duties; and
7. notify insurers or brokers within policy timescales.

Employees should not speculate about blame or promise compensation at the scene.

## Evidence preservation

Useful evidence may include photographs, damaged components, engineer notes, witness details, risk assessments, permits, test results, communications and equipment history.

Failed parts should not be discarded or returned to a supplier before responsibility for evidence has been considered. Where a warranty case and an insurance claim may involve the same incident, evidence requirements from [Warranty responsibility and remedial work](../warranty/warranty-responsibility-and-remedial-work.md) and insurance notification timescales must both be satisfied.

## Claims handling

Claims are coordinated through an authorised manager, with insurer, broker, legal adviser, customer and technical specialists involved as needed.

Frostline remains constructive with the customer while protecting its legal and insurance position. Referral to insurers does not justify silence or indifference.

## Near misses

Near misses are recorded where an event could reasonably have caused injury, damage or major disruption. They provide an opportunity to improve before a loss occurs.

A near miss is not made harmless merely because an engineer recovered the situation successfully.

## Risk ownership

Risks have owners responsible for monitoring and treatment. Ownership may sit with operations, finance, fleet, projects, compliance or directors depending on the subject.

Controls can include avoidance, reduction, transfer, contingency planning or conscious acceptance. Acceptance should be explicit where exposure is material.

## Business continuity

Frostline plans for disruptions such as:

- loss of premises;
- severe weather;
- extended power or communications failure;
- critical system outage;
- vehicle or fuel disruption;
- supplier failure;
- epidemic illness;
- loss of a key manager; and
- sudden demand surge.

Continuity arrangements prioritise safety, customer communication, critical service commitments, payroll and access to essential records.

## Financial risk

Large projects, slow-paying customers, deposits to suppliers and warranty exposure can create cash risk even where reported profit appears healthy.

Frostline monitors concentration by customer, aged debt, committed purchase cost, work in progress and contingent liabilities.

## Risk review

Management reviews significant risks and recent incidents periodically. Useful discussion focuses on changes in exposure and whether controls work in practice.

A long risk register with no decisions is administrative theatre. The purpose is to recognise uncertainty early enough to act.
