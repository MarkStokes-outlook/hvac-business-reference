# Manufacturer Relationships

## Overview

Manufacturers influence equipment selection, technical support, warranty outcomes, training, spare-parts availability and the long-term serviceability of installed systems. Frostline maintains practical working relationships with several manufacturers rather than depending on a single brand for every application.

Manufacturer relationships support engineering delivery, but they do not replace Frostline's responsibility to select suitable equipment, preserve evidence, control customer commitments and represent customer interests fairly.

This document follows [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md) and [Warranty responsibility and remedial work](../warranty/warranty-responsibility-and-remedial-work.md). Manufacturer advice, attendance or replacement-part approval does not automatically decide contractual responsibility, customer charging or Frostline expenditure.

## Relationship roles

The manufacturer, distributor and Frostline may each perform different roles:

- the manufacturer designs and supports the product;
- the distributor may sell, stock, deliver and process returns;
- Frostline selects, supplies, installs, commissions or maintains equipment for its customer;
- a consultant or customer may own part of the design basis; and
- a specialist service partner may perform authorised field work.

The commercial seller is not always the technical authority. The technical authority is not always responsible for labour or consequential cost. Records should identify which party is acting in which capacity.

## Approved installer programmes

Some manufacturers operate approved, accredited or partner installer schemes. Membership may require:

- technical training;
- minimum installation standards;
- evidence of insurance and certification;
- warranty registration performance;
- use of approved commissioning procedures;
- periodic assessment;
- minimum sales or purchase levels; and
- compliance with brand rules.

Programme status may provide extended warranty options, technical access, sales support or customer referrals. It should not be presented as independent proof that a product is suitable for every site or that every Frostline engineer is authorised on every product.

Programme conditions, renewal dates, engineer-level restrictions and consequences of lapse are retained where they affect customer commitments.

## Training and competence

Manufacturer training may cover installation, commissioning, fault diagnosis, controls, product updates and warranty processes. Frostline records which engineers have completed relevant training and whether refresher or product-specific learning is needed.

Attendance at a course does not by itself prove continuing competence. Experience, supervision, observed work quality and current product knowledge remain important.

A manufacturer certificate may support competence evidence but does not grant Frostline financial authority, customer authority or permission to work outside statutory certification.

## Product selection and application support

Manufacturer representatives can provide useful application advice, selections and performance data. Frostline evaluates recommendations against:

- the design basis and duty;
- site conditions and intended use;
- efficiency and refrigerant strategy;
- electrical and controls compatibility;
- noise and planning constraints;
- installation and maintenance access;
- parts and technical support;
- warranty conditions;
- lifecycle and end-of-life risk; and
- commercial lead time and availability.

Selection evidence should state the information supplied to the manufacturer and the assumptions behind its recommendation. A selection produced from incorrect loads or incomplete operating conditions is not validated merely because it carries a manufacturer logo.

Commercial incentives must not override technical suitability.

## Design responsibility

Manufacturer application support does not automatically make the manufacturer the system designer. Frostline identifies whether a manufacturer output is:

- published product data;
- an indicative selection;
- a project-specific selection based on supplied data;
- an engineered design deliverable;
- a controls sequence or configuration; or
- informal technical advice.

The project record states what Frostline has checked, what information was relied upon and who owns interfaces outside the manufacturer's scope.

Where advice appears unsafe, inconsistent or unsuitable, Frostline escalates rather than installing blindly.

## Technical support requests

Manufacturer technical support may assist with:

- product selection;
- design queries;
- wiring and controls;
- commissioning problems;
- diagnostic codes;
- software or firmware;
- unusual operating conditions;
- parts identification; and
- warranty assessment.

A useful support request includes:

- product model and serial number;
- site and system context;
- installation and commissioning date;
- exact symptom or alarm;
- state on arrival;
- relevant readings and test conditions;
- controls demand and sequence;
- work already attempted;
- photographs or diagrams;
- safety and operational status; and
- the specific question Frostline needs answered.

"It does not work" is not enough for effective technical assistance.

## Advice records and authority

Material manufacturer advice is recorded with:

- support case or reference;
- person or team providing advice;
- date and communication route;
- evidence supplied;
- advice received;
- conditions or caveats;
- tests or actions authorised;
- parts or software identified; and
- next action and owner.

The engineer on site remains responsible for local safety and must not follow advice they reasonably believe is unsafe or outside competence.

Manufacturer advice can inform a technical decision. It does not by itself:

- authorise customer expenditure;
- authorise Frostline purchasing;
- amend a contract;
- approve a variation;
- admit liability; or
- guarantee reimbursement.

## Field support engineers

Manufacturers may provide site attendance for complex diagnosis, commissioning support, quality investigation or product concerns. Attendance usually requires evidence that normal engineering checks have already been completed.

Before attendance, Frostline clarifies:

- the question or outcome required;
- whether the visit is advisory, diagnostic, commissioning or warranty-related;
- who controls site safety and permits;
- whether charges apply;
- what equipment or access must be available;
- who may alter settings or replace parts;
- evidence to be produced; and
- who communicates with the customer.

Responsibility for customer communication remains explicit. The customer should not be left uncertain about who owns the next action merely because a manufacturer has become involved.

## Warranty registration

Many equipment warranties depend on timely and accurate registration. Required information may include:

- installer details;
- installation and commissioning date;
- site and customer;
- model and serial numbers;
- commissioning readings;
- proof of purchase;
- service history; and
- evidence of approved installation.

Frostline distinguishes:

- registration required;
- registration submitted;
- registration accepted;
- evidence incomplete;
- registration rejected; and
- warranty conditions still requiring future maintenance or inspection.

Submission is not the same as acceptance. Late or incomplete registration can weaken a valid claim. Frostline treats registration as a controlled handover activity rather than optional administration.

## Suspected warranty cases

A customer report concerning recently supplied equipment creates a suspected warranty case. It does not prove a product defect or manufacturer responsibility.

Potential causes include:

- manufacturing defect;
- installation or commissioning error;
- contamination;
- unsuitable selection or design condition;
- power, controls, water or environmental issue;
- poor or missing maintenance;
- accidental or third-party damage;
- operation outside published limits;
- normal wear or consumable failure; and
- cause not yet established.

Frostline investigates evidence before assigning liability and follows the separate case controls in [Warranty responsibility and remedial work](../warranty/warranty-responsibility-and-remedial-work.md).

## Claim authority

A manufacturer may require prior approval before parts are changed, removed or returned. Frostline records whether approval covers:

- diagnosis only;
- replacement part supply;
- field labour;
- travel;
- refrigerant or consumables;
- access equipment;
- return carriage;
- specialist attendance; or
- a maximum authorised value.

A claim number alone is not proof that every cost is recoverable. Work outside the stated authorisation requires separate Frostline and customer decisions.

## Immediate restoration and evidence preservation

Where customer impact is serious, Frostline may restore service before a manufacturer reaches a final conclusion. Before intervention, the team considers whether it must preserve:

- failed components;
- controller logs;
- photographs;
- packaging and delivery evidence;
- oil, refrigerant or other samples;
- electrical protection or power-quality evidence;
- settings and configuration; and
- commissioning history.

The record explains why immediate action was necessary and whether it may affect later investigation or recovery.

A fast repair that destroys the only claim evidence can transfer a valid manufacturer cost back to Frostline.

## Labour and warranty recovery

Manufacturer warranty may cover replacement parts without covering all Frostline labour, travel, access equipment, refrigerant, freight or consequential work. The customer contract and manufacturer terms determine how cost is treated.

Frostline records separately:

- gross cost incurred;
- customer charge;
- manufacturer claim value;
- supplier or distributor credit;
- subcontract recovery;
- goodwill contribution;
- amount received; and
- final unrecovered cost.

A promised replacement or credit is not treated as recovered value until confirmed and processed.

## Returns and RMAs

Returned-material-authorisation processes may require a reference, packaging, failure description and return within a defined period. Credit can be refused where parts are damaged, incomplete, contaminated, lost or returned without approval.

Removed components are labelled with the related customer, site, asset, job and claim. Custody and disposal state remain visible until the return, investigation or authorised disposal is complete.

Returned evidence must not be mixed with scrap merely because the remedial attendance has finished.

## Defective products and quality reporting

Where Frostline sees recurring failures or unusual behaviour, it records affected models, serial ranges, symptoms, operating conditions, installation context and corrective action. Evidence is escalated as a potential pattern rather than treated as unrelated individual jobs.

A pattern may lead to:

- technical bulletins;
- revised installation guidance;
- parts changes;
- enhanced commissioning checks;
- temporary sales restrictions;
- customer inspections;
- formal recall action; or
- removal from the preferred-product list.

An unresolved pattern has an owner and review date. Brand loyalty is not a reason to suppress adverse evidence.

## Product recalls and safety notices

Manufacturers may issue recalls, safety notices or mandatory modifications. Frostline assesses:

- authenticity and scope of the notice;
- affected product, model and serial population;
- customers, sites and assets potentially affected;
- risk level and required timescale;
- interim operating restrictions;
- parts and labour arrangements;
- communication responsibility;
- access and scheduling dependencies;
- commercial recovery; and
- evidence of completion.

Urgent safety action is prioritised even where commercial responsibility is still being resolved.

A recall campaign is controlled as a portfolio of linked affected assets and actions, not a loose collection of emails.

## Software and firmware

Modern controls and connected equipment may depend on software or firmware. Updates can correct faults, add compatibility or address security concerns, but may also alter behaviour.

Before a significant update, Frostline considers:

- reason and authority;
- current and target version;
- release notes and known issues;
- configuration backup;
- controls and network dependencies;
- downtime and rollback;
- recommissioning requirements;
- customer or controls-contractor involvement; and
- warranty or support implications.

The update and resulting tests are recorded. An undocumented firmware change can make future diagnosis and responsibility assessment materially harder.

## Spare parts and supersession

Manufacturer support is judged partly by:

- accuracy of parts identification;
- stock availability;
- lead time;
- distribution network;
- technical documentation;
- supersession information;
- compatibility evidence; and
- support for older products.

A superseded part is not assumed equivalent solely because it replaces an old number in a catalogue. Frostline confirms any required wiring, controls, mounting, firmware or commissioning change.

A low equipment purchase price can be outweighed by poor parts availability over the asset life.

## Lead times and allocation

Equipment and parts lead times are confirmed for specific orders rather than assumed from historic experience. Scarcity, production changes and seasonal demand can affect availability.

Frostline distinguishes indicative lead time, supplier acknowledgement, manufacturer allocation and confirmed dispatch. Only the latter stages provide a strong basis for customer commitments.

Where manufacturers allocate constrained stock, Frostline avoids promising customer dates until supply is reasonably secure and records the risk where dates remain conditional.

## Merchant and distributor relationships

Many products are purchased through merchants or specialist distributors. These relationships affect pricing, credit, local stock, delivery and returns.

The distributor may be the commercial route while the manufacturer remains the technical or warranty authority. Frostline identifies:

- who accepted the purchase order;
- who owns delivery performance;
- who authorises returns;
- who decides technical warranty;
- who issues credit; and
- who owes Frostline the recovery.

Responsibilities should not become blurred when a problem occurs.

## Commercial agreements and rebates

Manufacturer agreements may include project pricing, volume discounts, rebates, demonstration equipment or marketing support. Frostline records the conditions, earning period, exclusions and confidence of recovery.

Pricing decisions do not treat uncertain future rebate as guaranteed margin. Commercial support must not distort technical advice or create undisclosed customer conflicts.

## Marketing support

Joint case studies, referrals and branded material may be useful where claims are accurate and the customer has consented. Frostline does not allow manufacturer marketing to overstate performance, conceal operating limitations or imply endorsements that do not exist.

## Product lifecycle and end of life

Manufacturers may discontinue equipment, refrigerants, controls platforms or spare parts. Frostline monitors lifecycle information where it affects maintained customers.

End-of-life status does not always require immediate replacement, but it changes risk. Customers may need advice on:

- parts scarcity;
- support limitations;
- firmware or controls obsolescence;
- refrigerant implications;
- repair-versus-replace economics;
- phased replacement; and
- compatibility with remaining systems.

Advice distinguishes confirmed manufacturer status from Frostline's own risk assessment.

## Manufacturer performance review

Frostline reviews manufacturers and principal distributors using evidence such as:

- product reliability;
- application and selection quality;
- technical response quality;
- warranty acceptance and recovery;
- parts accuracy and availability;
- delivery reliability;
- training value;
- lifecycle support;
- communication during serious incidents; and
- commercial fairness.

Preferred status may be narrowed by product family, application or geography rather than applied to an entire brand.

## Escalation and relationship ownership

Frostline maintains named sales, technical, warranty and service contacts where possible. Significant unresolved issues are escalated with:

- a clear question or required outcome;
- chronology;
- supporting evidence;
- customer and operational impact;
- cost exposure;
- previous responses;
- required decision date; and
- Frostline owner.

A friendly relationship is useful, but it is not a control. Unresolved manufacturer actions remain visible until accepted, concluded, transferred or deliberately written off under authority.
