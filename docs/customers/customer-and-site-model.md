# Customer and Site Model

## Purpose

Frostline's work is organised around commercial relationships, physical sites and installed equipment. Those concepts are related but not interchangeable. A customer may operate many sites, a site may be occupied by a different organisation from the one paying Frostline, and equipment at one site may be covered by several contracts or warranties.

This document describes the business concepts used by Frostline staff when discussing customers and delivery locations. It does not prescribe a software data model. It uses the record meanings in [Canonical terminology and record relationships](../governance/canonical-terminology-and-record-relationships.md) and the authority distinctions in [Decision rights and operational authority](../governance/decision-rights-and-operational-authority.md).

## Customer organisations

A customer is an organisation with which Frostline has a commercial relationship. The customer may request quotations, place orders, enter into maintenance agreements, receive invoices or authorise work.

Common customer types include:

- owner-operated businesses;
- property owners and landlords;
- managing agents;
- facilities-management providers;
- academy trusts and other public-sector bodies;
- care and supported-living groups;
- hospitality and leisure operators; and
- regional businesses with multiple branches or trading sites.

The name used by staff is often the familiar trading name rather than the full legal entity. Finance staff need the legal entity and billing details, while engineers and coordinators usually recognise the site or trading name first.

## Commercial relationships

A single organisation can have more than one commercial arrangement with Frostline. For example, a hotel group may hold a maintenance contract for twelve sites while also commissioning a separate replacement project at one hotel.

Different people within the same customer organisation may perform different roles:

- commercial or procurement contacts approve suppliers and prices;
- facilities contacts report faults and coordinate access;
- site managers confirm local arrangements;
- finance contacts receive invoices and statements;
- technical contacts review designs or compliance evidence; and
- senior sponsors handle escalations and relationship matters.

The person who reports a fault may not be authorised to approve expenditure. The person who approves expenditure may never visit the affected site.

## Sites

A site is a physical location where Frostline surveys, installs, services or repairs equipment. It normally has a postal address, local access arrangements and one or more operational contacts.

Sites vary considerably. A site may be:

- a single retail unit;
- a school campus containing several buildings;
- a hotel with guest, kitchen and plant areas;
- an office building occupied by several tenants;
- a warehouse and attached offices;
- a care facility operating continuously; or
- one branch within a regional customer estate.

A customer can own, occupy, manage or merely procure services for a site. Frostline therefore distinguishes the commercial customer from the local site identity, even when staff use the same name for both in conversation.

## Site contacts and access

Each site can have several relevant contacts. Site contacts may include a manager, caretaker, security desk, maintenance person, tenant representative or out-of-hours keyholder.

Access information can include:

- normal opening hours;
- delivery restrictions;
- parking or loading arrangements;
- permit or induction requirements;
- roof, plant-room or ceiling-void access;
- alarm and keyholding procedures;
- asbestos-register location;
- safeguarding requirements; and
- restrictions on noisy, dusty or disruptive work.

Access arrangements are operationally important and can change without the commercial customer formally notifying Frostline. Coordinators often confirm them again before attendance.

## Customer estate structure

Multi-site customers usually group sites informally by region, brand, contract or operating division. Frostline accommodates those groupings in account management and reporting, but they are not always stable.

One customer may ask for:

- one purchase order covering all sites;
- separate purchase orders per site;
- consolidated monthly invoicing;
- invoices addressed to local cost centres;
- regional approval thresholds; or
- performance reporting split by brand or contract.

These arrangements are agreed commercially and may differ between planned maintenance, reactive work and projects.

## Contracting entity and billing entity

The familiar trading name used by staff does not always identify the legal entity that is contractually liable and expected to pay. A customer group may contain several registered companies, and invoices addressed to the wrong entity can be rejected on administrative grounds regardless of service quality.

Finance requires the correct legal entity, company registration details, VAT number and billing address for each contracting arrangement. Where a facilities manager, managing agent or procurement body places orders on behalf of another party, Frostline establishes which entity is the payer and whether the intermediary has authority to bind them.

## Equipment ownership

Equipment installed at a site is not necessarily owned by the site occupier. Ownership may rest with a landlord, tenant, leasing provider or another group company. Frostline normally works to the authority of the customer placing the order and records ownership only where it affects warranty, replacement approval or contract responsibility.

## Relationship knowledge

Important customer knowledge is distributed across the business. Sales staff understand commercial history, coordinators know day-to-day preferences, engineers know the condition of sites and equipment, and finance staff understand billing behaviour.

Frostline's best relationships depend on combining those perspectives. The company does not regard a customer as merely an account name or invoice address; it is an evolving network of people, sites, agreements, expectations and prior work.
