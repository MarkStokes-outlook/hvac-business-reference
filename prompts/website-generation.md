# Website Generation Assignment

## Objective

Create the complete public website for Frostline Mechanical Services Ltd for the benchmark that supplies this assignment.

The repository describes the complete internal business. The benchmark's public-brand material defines how that business is projected externally for this scenario. Use both sources together:

1. `docs/` defines what Frostline is and what is factually supportable;
2. the benchmark's `public-brand/` directory defines what should be publicly visible, how the company is positioned and what level of presentation is appropriate; and
3. the generated website is an artefact derived from those sources, not an additional source of business truth.

Do not assume that one website applies to every benchmark. Different benchmarks may intentionally represent different public projections, branding choices, information availability or points in the company's development.

This is intended to be a one-pass generation task. Inspect the controlling material carefully, make proportionate creative decisions and deliver a complete result without relying on conversational follow-up to fill ordinary design gaps.

## Mental model

Treat the repository as the company's ERP, CRM, quality system and operations manual.

Treat the benchmark public-brand brief as the instructions given to its marketing supplier.

Treat the website as the company's brochure.

The website is a curated public representation of the business, not a publication of all internal knowledge.

## Public information boundary

Include only information that would reasonably appear on the public website of a mature regional UK HVAC engineering company and that is appropriate to the benchmark's public-brand brief.

Public-facing material may include, where supported and relevant:

- services and customer outcomes;
- sectors served;
- geographic coverage;
- company history and values;
- planned-maintenance and reactive-service propositions;
- installation and project capability;
- environmental or technical capability;
- recruitment and career information;
- contact routes and calls to action; and
- accreditations, certifications or guarantees only where explicitly evidenced.

Do not expose internal operational knowledge merely because it exists in the repository. In particular, do not publish internal:

- workflows and state models;
- approval thresholds or delegated authority;
- dispatch and scheduling rules;
- engineer competence matrices;
- escalation routes;
- commercial decision processes;
- contract administration controls;
- governance documents;
- benchmark mechanics, rubrics or reference reasoning; or
- unresolved internal weaknesses that would not normally be disclosed publicly.

Internal knowledge may inform accurate public copy without being reproduced. For example, a detailed service process may support a concise customer-facing statement about organised delivery, but the internal process itself should remain internal.

## Marketing role

Act as an experienced B2B marketing agency specialising in regional engineering, building-services and industrial-service businesses.

Your job is to select, synthesise and present the business in a way that:

- attracts appropriate prospective customers;
- reassures facilities, operations and procurement audiences;
- communicates practical customer value;
- makes the business easy to understand and contact; and
- feels commercially persuasive without becoming exaggerated or false.

Marketing language may improve clarity, readability, emphasis and customer appeal. It must not introduce unsupported factual claims.

Do not reproduce canonical wording mechanically. Translate internal truth into natural public-facing language while preserving the underlying meaning. Optimise for plausible marketing communication, not documentary completeness.

## Authority and factual controls

The repository remains the sole authority for enduring business facts. The benchmark public-brand brief may select, omit, frame or emphasise those facts, but must not override them.

Do not invent:

- services or products;
- named customers, case studies or testimonials;
- certifications or accreditations;
- quantitative KPIs or market-share claims;
- prices or contractual entitlements;
- guarantees, response commitments or availability promises;
- legal claims or regulatory status;
- company history beyond what is documented; or
- contact details not supplied by the benchmark or repository.

Do not manufacture trust devices merely because a template normally contains them. Unsupported counters, awards, customer logos, review scores, testimonial carousels, membership badges and claims such as "trusted by hundreds" must be omitted.

If a public page normally requires information that is absent, omit the claim, use an honest generic treatment, or implement the page without fabricated specifics. Do not expose the absence as an internal documentation problem on the public site.

Where the benchmark explicitly permits placeholders for contact or legal values, keep them clearly configurable and visually unobtrusive. Do not convert placeholders into fictional facts.

## Brand realism

Create branding that is typical and credible for this kind of company.

Assume Frostline commissioned a competent local web-design agency, freelance designer or mainstream website-builder specialist rather than a globally recognised brand studio.

The visual identity should feel:

- professional;
- established;
- practical;
- trustworthy;
- clean;
- recognisably associated with heating, cooling, ventilation or engineering; and
- suitable for vans, workwear, quotations and site signage.

Avoid branding that feels unusually fashionable, conceptual, luxurious, disruptive, startup-like or technology-company inspired unless the benchmark public-brand brief explicitly calls for it.

A conventional name treatment, straightforward logo device, limited colour palette and familiar engineering imagery are entirely acceptable. Authenticity matters more than novelty.

## Website realism

The implementation quality should match what this business would plausibly commission in reality.

The finished site should resemble a good implementation produced using a mainstream WordPress theme, Squarespace, Wix, Webflow template or comparable commercial website-builder product. It may look polished and modern, but it should not resemble an award-winning digital-agency showcase or a venture-funded software product.

Prefer familiar patterns such as:

- a clear header and conventional navigation;
- strong hero messaging and practical calls to action;
- service cards;
- sector or capability sections;
- trust-building company content;
- restrained use of icons and engineering photography;
- straightforward enquiry forms; and
- a conventional footer.

Avoid unnecessary animation, experimental interaction, cinematic transitions, excessive motion, highly bespoke editorial layouts or technical flourishes that would be implausible for a regional HVAC contractor.

Do not overuse generic template devices. KPI counters, oversized statistics, floating badges, decorative dashboards and startup-style feature grids should appear only when supported by the business and appropriate to the brief.

Optimise for plausibility rather than impressiveness.

## Imagery and visual asset requirements

Do not leave large image placeholders, grey boxes or text labels such as `[Image: ...]` in the finished website when suitable visual assets can reasonably be created or sourced within the available environment.

Create or source a small, coherent image library that supports the benchmark's public brand. Prefer a realistic set of reusable images over a different unrelated image for every section.

Appropriate subjects may include, where consistent with the brief:

- engineers inspecting or servicing commercial HVAC equipment;
- rooftop plant and packaged units;
- commercial plant rooms;
- controls panels or diagnostic work;
- engineers arriving at or working on commercial premises;
- offices, retail, hospitality, education or light-industrial buildings;
- team or customer-discussion scenes that do not imply named real customers; and
- vans, workwear or signage using only the generated Frostline brand.

Visual assets should:

- feel like commercial photography commissioned or licensed by a regional engineering company;
- use a consistent photographic and colour treatment;
- show believable UK commercial environments where geography is visible;
- portray safe, professional working practices;
- avoid legible third-party logos, customer identities and recognisable protected brands;
- avoid implying certifications, partnerships, customers, projects or capabilities not supported by the repository; and
- avoid obvious AI artefacts, impossible equipment layouts or unsafe engineering scenes.

If image generation or suitable asset sourcing is genuinely unavailable, use polished abstract brand graphics, restrained engineering-themed illustrations, gradients, icons or CSS compositions instead of unfinished placeholder boxes. Record that limitation in the artefact README, not in customer-facing copy.

Do not generate image quantity for its own sake. A believable small-business site may reuse a coherent library of approximately five to ten strong assets across pages.

## Audience

Primary audiences include:

- facilities managers;
- operations managers;
- commercial property owners;
- procurement teams;
- principal contractors and consultants;
- existing customers; and
- prospective customers.

The website should build confidence through clarity, competence and professionalism.

## Style

Use public-facing copy that is:

- professional;
- clear;
- credible;
- concise;
- commercial;
- human; and
- appropriately confident.

Avoid generic AI phrasing, inflated superlatives, empty marketing clichés and internal terminology that would mean little to a customer.

## Deliverable

Produce a complete, realistic website with:

- an appropriate information architecture;
- navigation and page hierarchy;
- finished public copy;
- calls to action;
- responsive presentation;
- coherent benchmark-specific branding;
- a finished and consistent visual asset treatment;
- suitable accessibility fundamentals; and
- legal or privacy pages only to the level supportable without inventing legal facts.

Implementation technology is not prescribed. Choose a proportionate, maintainable approach and avoid introducing a complex software-engineering architecture merely to build a conventional brochure website.

Do not expose the repository's document structure in the finished site. When several documents contribute to a page, synthesise them naturally as though a marketing supplier had interviewed Frostline's leadership and operational teams.

The delivered artefact must not contain unfinished design scaffolding, visible TODOs, lorem ipsum, image-description placeholders or temporary copy. Configuration placeholders explicitly allowed by the benchmark are the only exception.

## Validation

Before declaring the website complete:

- inspect every page at desktop and mobile widths;
- verify navigation, internal links, calls to action and forms;
- check that visual assets load correctly and have useful alternative text;
- confirm no unsupported facts, claims, logos or identities have been introduced;
- confirm no internal governance or benchmark material has leaked into public pages;
- remove unused assets, dead code and obvious template remnants; and
- record implementation choices, controlling sources, validation performed and any genuine environment limitations in the artefact README.

## Provenance

Include a discreet provenance note outside the fictional company's normal marketing journey, such as in the site footer or a dedicated demonstration page. It should explain that:

- Frostline is fictional;
- the website was generated from the canonical business knowledge in this repository;
- the benchmark's public-brand brief controlled the public projection; and
- no additional business facts were supplied during generation.

The provenance note must not make the main customer-facing pages feel like repository documentation.
